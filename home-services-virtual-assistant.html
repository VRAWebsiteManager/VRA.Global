FAQ

Questions Contractors Ask

What a VRA assistant covers on the phones, in the schedule, and through invoicing — and what it costs.

What does a home services virtual assistant do?

Booking and schedule support: answering and returning customer calls during your business hours, booking jobs and estimates into your scheduling software, dispatch confirmations, reminders and reschedules, following up on unsold estimates and expired quotes, keeping crew calendars and job notes current, and maintenance-plan and seasonal service reminders. Back-office support: invoicing, payment reminders and calls on past-due invoices, bid and estimate preparation, writing down how the office runs as SOPs your team can follow, permit paperwork, warranty registrations and job documentation, supplier and material order coordination, CRM management, review requests and reputation monitoring, and social media and local marketing support.

Can an assistant answer the phone when our crews are on a job?

Yes — answering and returning customer calls during your business hours is core to the role, along with booking the work straight into your scheduling software. Missed calls are usually the most expensive problem in a trades business, and they are the first thing an assistant fixes.

Will they chase unpaid invoices and unsold estimates?

Yes. Invoicing, payment reminders and calls on your own past-due invoices, plus follow-up on unsold estimates and expired quotes, are standard back-office work for a VRA assistant.

Can an assistant cover after-hours calls?

Shifts outside standard business hours can be arranged, evenings and overnight included. An emergency call answered at eleven at night is usually the difference between the job and the next contractor on the list, so tell us what the cover needs to look like and we will tell you what we can schedule. The rate is the same whatever hours the role runs, with no after-hours upcharge.

Our customers and crews speak Spanish. Can an assistant handle that?

Yes. VRA places Spanish–English bilingual assistants at the same flat rate as any other assistant — no upcharge for bilingual talent — so the calls, the scheduling and the follow-up can happen in whichever language your customer is comfortable with.

What stays with our team?

VRA virtual assistants provide administrative, marketing, and customer support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal, financial, or other advice requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by you and are subject to your own security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.

We need office help but cannot afford a full-time hire. Where do we start?

Start with the phone. A missed call is a lost job and you never find out which ones they were, so it is the most expensive gap on the list and the easiest to close. Part-time is $800 a month for 20 hours a week; full-time is $1,500 for 40. You can start at twenty hours, and increased coverage is available at any time. If you are weighing it against a payroll hire, we set the two side by side.
