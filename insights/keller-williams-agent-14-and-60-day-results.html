14 Days, 60 Days: What Two Keller Williams Agents Say About Their VRA Virtual Assistant

Every VRA match comes with a built-in check-in schedule — 14, 30, 60, and 90 days after a virtual assistant starts — specifically so a Keller Williams agent isn't just taking VRA's word for it that the fit is working. Instead of describing what those check-ins are supposed to accomplish, here's what two real Keller Williams agents actually said at two of those milestones.

14 Days In: Kaylee Fisher, Keller Williams Elite

Kaylee Fisher is a REALTOR® with Keller Williams Elite in Texas. Two weeks after her VRA virtual assistant started, she sat down for VRA's standard 14-day check-in — the earliest of the four milestones VRA builds into every match, meant to catch a bad fit quickly and confirm a good one just as fast. Her answer:

“For the first time in months, I have been able to cook dinner for my family. I have been able to go to some of my son's baseball practices.”
— Kaylee Fisher, REALTOR®, Keller Williams Elite

Fourteen days is early — a new VA is still coming up to speed on an agent's specific workflow. What a result at that stage usually reflects isn't a fully optimized business yet; it's the first sign that recurring admin, the kind that quietly eats evenings and weekends, has actually started moving off an agent's plate.

60 Days In: Emily Zakhem, Keller Williams Memorial

Emily Zakhem is a REALTOR® with Keller Williams Memorial, recognized as a Top 20% producer and Luxury Agent. By her 60-day check-in — the milestone VRA uses to gauge whether a VA has become a genuine fixture in an agent's business rather than a short trial run — her answer was direct:

“I cannot think of running my business without a virtual assistant.”
— Emily Zakhem, REALTOR®, Keller Williams Memorial (Top 20%, Luxury Agent)

Sixty days is enough time for a VA to move past onboarding and into the actual rhythm of an agent's production — particularly for a top-producing, luxury-focused agent, where deal volume and client communication typically run well above average.

Why VRA Checks In at 14, 30, 60, and 90 Days

The check-in schedule isn't a formality — it's built to remove the risk most agents associate with hiring a VA sight unseen. Every VRA engagement comes with no long trial window and no fine print: if a VA isn't the right fit at any point, you can swap in a different VA at no extra cost. The 14-day check-in catches an obvious mismatch early; the 30- and 60-day check-ins confirm the VA has learned an agent's actual workflow; and the 90-day check-in is typically where the relationship stops being an experiment and starts being infrastructure.
