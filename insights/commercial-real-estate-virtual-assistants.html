How Commercial Real Estate Agents Use a Virtual Assistant

A good year in commercial real estate creates its own problem. The listings you won six months ago all need working at once, and the hours that go into working them are the same hours that were supposed to go into winning the next ones. Nothing has gone wrong. You are just about to spend a strong year standing still.

This is an account of what two commercial agents actually handed to an assistant, taken from their own check-ins rather than from a list of services. They are two of the commercial and business brokerage practices VRA places with, alongside Keller Williams Commercial and Brockway Commercial.

Month One Was Not About Listings

Melanne Carpenter is a commercial agent at KW Commercial. At her 60-day check-in, the first thing she described was not deal support:

“So the first month he focused on getting me organized, because that was really kind of my weak spot. And then now the second month, he is really focusing on the listings I have. I have the most listings I’ve ever had.”

That order matters, and it is the part most people get backwards. The instinct is to hand over the visible work first — the follow-up, the marketing, the thing that is currently late. What actually happened was a month spent on organization, and only then the listings. An assistant working inside a disorganized system spends their time asking you where things are, which is slower than doing it yourself.

Month Two Was the Pipeline

Once the system was in order, the work moved to the thing a strong year quietly threatens:

“And so now he is doing the follow-ups for me so that we can keep the pipeline going. So it’s great to have all these listings, but they’re going to close at some point. And so he’s focused on that while I’m focusing on the next round of business.”

That last sentence is the whole arrangement in one line. She is not delegating to get her evenings back. She is splitting the work so that servicing this year’s listings and generating next year’s stop competing for the same person. In a market with twelve- and eighteen-month cycles, that competition is what caps a commercial practice, and it does it silently — there is no bad month to point at, just a flat year after a good one.

Her summary was short: “And it has been life-changing and business changing.”

Wondering what you would hand over first? The Needs Assessment takes about two minutes and comes back with a specific recommendation.

A Second Case: Business Brokerage and Commercial

Alexandra Pocora’s practice spans business brokerage and commercial, which Michael noted on camera is different from the work VRA assistants usually take on. Her answer on what her assistant handles was characteristically brief:

“He is helping us with organization of documents in the system, marketing-wise.”

Document organization again, before anything else. In commercial and brokerage files that is not clerical work — it is the difference between a diligence request answered the same day and one that takes a week while somebody hunts through folders.

Asked whether her assistant was reliable, she said something more revealing than a testimonial usually manages:

“Yes, always. Even if we are not — maybe sometimes we are late, but he’s always on time. Every morning, 8:30, good morning.”

What Commercial Teams Hand Over

Across both, and consistent with what the commercial real estate virtual assistant page sets out, the work divides like this:

  • Document control across long-cycle files — the version everyone is working from, where it lives, who has it, with signatures through DocuSign or Authentisign
  • Listing follow-up and pipeline movement, so deals in flight keep moving while you source new ones
  • Property and market research in CoStar, pulled and formatted before you need it
  • Marketing: listing collateral, email campaigns, the collateral nobody finishes on a busy week
  • CRM upkeep in RealNex, plus KW Command and LoopNet listing updates — records that survive an eighteen-month cycle
  • Administrative underwriting support — gathering and organizing inputs, not forming the view

Judgment, valuation and anything requiring a license stay with the agent. That is not a hedge; it is what makes the rest delegable. An assistant who is clear about the line can be given more, not less.

Why the First Month Looks Slow

Both check-ins describe the same opening move, and it is worth planning for rather than being surprised by. The first month goes on organization: where documents live, how files are named, what the follow-up cadence is, which of your habits are judgment and which are just reflex.

That month feels like it is not producing anything. It is the month that decides whether month two produces anything. We commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you, but system proficiency and knowing your practice are different things, and the second one is what Melanne was describing.

If you have never written down how your files actually move, there is a longer piece on writing procedures people follow, and it is the single thing that makes month one shorter.

Commercial Real Estate Virtual Assistants: Common Questions

Why does the first month with a commercial assistant feel slow?

Because it goes on organization rather than on anything visible. Both commercial clients whose check-ins are on camera describe the same opening month: document control, file naming, where things live, what the follow-up cadence is. It looks like it is not producing anything, and it is the month that decides whether the second one does.

What systems do commercial real estate assistants work in?

Assistants placed with Keller Williams commercial agents work in CoStar for property and market data, LoopNet for listings, KW Command for contacts, tasks and documents, RealNex as the CRM, and DocuSign or Authentisign for signatures. They work in your own accounts at the permission level you set. If your team runs something else, we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

Can an assistant handle listing follow-up while I source new business?

That is the most common arrangement. One commercial agent described it directly at her 60-day check-in: her assistant took the follow-ups so the pipeline kept moving while she focused on the next round of business. Splitting it that way is what stops a strong year from becoming a flat one, because servicing current listings and sourcing new ones otherwise compete for the same hours.

What did a commercial agent actually hand over in the first 60 days?

Month one was organization, which she described as her weak spot. Month two was the listings: follow-up on everything in flight so the pipeline kept moving. At the 60-day mark she reported having the most listings she had ever had, with her assistant working the follow-up while she worked the next round of business.

Should I write my processes down before an assistant starts?

It is the single thing that shortens the first month. If how your files move exists only in your head, the assistant is learning the process and performing it at the same time, and every exception comes back to you. Writing it down first, or having the assistant write it down by watching in week one, turns that month into a shorter one.

Does this work for business brokerage as well as commercial?

Yes. One of the two clients described here runs business brokerage alongside commercial, and the work is the same shape: document organization across long files, marketing, and follow-up that has to survive a cycle measured in months rather than weeks.
