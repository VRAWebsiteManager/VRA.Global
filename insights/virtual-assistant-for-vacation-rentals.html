Virtual Assistant for Vacation Rentals: What They Do Across the Guest Cycle

The short answer: a vacation rental virtual assistant works the sequence around the stay — the inquiry that needs an answer before the guest books somewhere else, the confirmation and pre-arrival details, the in-stay questions that are not emergencies, the turnover between guests, the review request afterwards, and the listing, rate and calendar updates that keep all of it accurate across Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com and your own site. This guide follows one booking from first message to the review, and shows where an assistant picks it up.

Where Does Rental Admin Actually Break?

A rental business rarely falls apart at hosting. It gets away from you in the gaps: the inquiry that lands while you are on the phone with a plumber, the pre-arrival message you meant to send from the car, the review window that closes while you are turning over the next guest. None of it is difficult work. All of it is time-sensitive, and it arrives on somebody else’s schedule.

It also does not scale in the direction you would hope. Two properties is a handful of messages a day. Five is a job. And a guest who books on Friday for Saturday needs the same four touches as one who books six months out, compressed into an afternoon.

That sequence is what a vacation rental assistant takes over. Not the hosting — the admin wrapped around it.

The Inquiry, Before They Book Somewhere Else

This is the one stage where minutes change the outcome. Someone comparing listings is rarely waiting on yours alone, and most platforms factor response rate and response time into how a listing surfaces in search, so a slow inbox costs you the booking and then some visibility on the next one.

At this stage your assistant is in the inbox across the channels you sell on and the form on your own site, and works it the same way each time:

  • Answers the standard questions from replies you have already approved — parking, pets, check-in timing, the hot tub, whether the loft counts as a bedroom
  • Checks the calendar and quotes the dates, including the gap night nobody has filled
  • Flags the ones that need you: a long stay at a discount, a group that reads like a party, a date that collides with an owner booking
  • Follows up on the inquiries that went quiet, once, with something more useful than a nudge

Agencies in this space promise fast inquiry response, and they are right to. What is worth being specific about is which hours: your assistant works the schedule you set, and shifts outside standard business hours, evenings and overnight included, can be arranged at the same flat rate with no after-hours upcharge.

Before the Stay

Between the booking and the arrival sits a short sequence that is easy to do and easy to forget. The confirmation. The pre-arrival message with directions, parking and the door code timed the way your lock requires. The rental agreement or deposit if you use one. The guest-screening step if your platform or your insurer asks for it. The change requests — early check-in, an extra guest, a cot, a dog that was not mentioned at booking.

An assistant runs that on a schedule rather than from memory: the confirmation the day it books, the details a few days out, the code when it should go. The difference an owner notices first is not that the messages are better written. It is that the guest arrives already knowing where to park.

During the Stay: The Messages That Are Not Emergencies

Most in-stay messages are not emergencies. The wifi password that is on the fridge. Which day the trash goes out. Where the extra towels live. How the thermostat works. A dinner recommendation. They arrive at seven in the evening, and they are the reason hosts stop enjoying hosting.

Your assistant answers those from the house guide you have written, and escalates what actually needs a person — a leak, a lockout, a neighbor complaint — to you or to whoever you have on the ground, with enough detail to act on instead of a forwarded screenshot. When something does go wrong, the documentation gets assembled while it is still fresh: the message thread, the photos, the timeline, the maintenance ticket. Filing the claim is yours; having the file ready is the part that usually does not happen.

The Turnover

Between stays the work is scheduling and confirmation, and the failure mode is always the same: the cleaner was booked against last week’s calendar. An assistant schedules cleaners and maintenance against the live calendar, moves the clean when a late checkout is approved, orders supplies before the shelf is empty, and confirms the turnover happened before the next guest is at the door — with photo confirmation from the cleaner if you want it, so you know the property was ready without driving over to look.

Owner stays, blocked dates and maintenance windows go on the same calendar, which is how double bookings stop happening.

After the Stay: Reviews, Repeat Guests and the Listing

The work after checkout is the work that compounds, and it is the first thing to get dropped. Your assistant sends the review request inside the window, drafts replies to the reviews that come back for your approval, and keeps the guests who said they would come back every summer from going quiet — a note ahead of the season they travel, where the platform’s terms allow direct contact.

Then there is the listing maintenance nobody schedules. Photos after you replaced the furniture. The description that still mentions the grill you got rid of. Seasonal rates, minimum-night rules, the calendar synced across channels. It is not urgent on any particular day, which is exactly why it drifts.

Rates, Availability and the Channels

Pricing is the one place to be precise about who does what. Your assistant executes the pricing rules you set — seasonal rates, weekend premiums, minimum stays, gap-night discounts — across the channels and your own site, and reports back on occupancy, inquiry volume and response times so you can see what the rules are doing. Choosing the strategy stays with you, which is the arrangement most owners want anyway.

The system you already run is the one your assistant works in: Guesty, Hostaway or OwnerRez on the rental side, the channels you distribute through — Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com among them — and QuickBooks for the books and owner statements. Assistants are trained on transferable fundamentals rather than one vendor’s product, which is why a new tool is a few days rather than a new hire.

Questions Vacation Rental Owners Ask

What does a vacation rental virtual assistant do?

They work the guest cycle for your properties: watching the inquiry inbox across Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking.com and your own site and answering from the replies you have approved, checking the calendar and quoting dates, sending confirmations and pre-arrival details, handling change requests, answering in-stay questions from your house guide and escalating anything that needs someone on the ground, scheduling cleaners and maintenance between stays and confirming the turnover happened, then requesting the review, drafting replies to reviews, and keeping listings, rates, minimum stays and the calendar current across channels. Bookkeeping support in QuickBooks and owner reporting sit alongside it.

Can an assistant handle guest messages on Airbnb, Vrbo and Booking.com?

Yes, and it is usually the first thing an owner hands over. Messages are worked in the platform itself or in the property management system that pulls them together — Guesty, Hostaway and OwnerRez among them — so the thread stays where the platform expects it and your response history stays intact. Your assistant answers the standard questions from the replies you have approved and brings you the ones that need a decision: a discount on a long stay, a group that reads like a party, a date that collides with an owner booking.

How quickly does a booking inquiry need an answer?

Fast enough that the guest has not booked something else. Someone comparing listings is rarely waiting on yours alone, so the practical target is the same day, and inside the hour during the hours you staff. Most platforms also factor response rate and response time into how a listing surfaces in search, so a slow inbox costs you the booking and then the visibility. An assistant watching the inbox through the day is what makes a quick answer routine rather than lucky, and shifts outside standard business hours can be arranged at the same flat rate with no after-hours upcharge if your inquiries cluster in the evening.

Can one assistant manage several properties?

Yes. At one or two properties the guest messaging is steady rather than heavy, so twenty hours a week covers a great deal of it. As a portfolio grows, what fills the time first is turnover coordination and listing maintenance rather than messaging. Part-time is $800 a month for 20 hours a week and full-time is $1,500 for 40, flat either way, so adding coverage is a known number rather than a per-property fee.

What account access does an assistant need?

Whatever you decide to give them. In practice that is a team-member or sub-account seat on the platforms you sell on or in your property management system, the shared inbox, and the calendar. VRA virtual assistants provide administrative, marketing, and customer support under the direction of the client, and access and permissions are determined by you and are subject to your own security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures. We will have an assistant sign an NDA whenever it is requested.

Can an assistant reply to a bad review?

They can draft the reply and send it once you have approved it. A poor review is read by the next several people weighing your property, which makes the reply matter more than the review; short, specific and not defensive is what works. Your assistant also assembles what you need to answer accurately — the message thread, the cleaner’s turnover photos, the maintenance ticket — so the response is built on what happened rather than what anyone remembers.
