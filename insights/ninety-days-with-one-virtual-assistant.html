Ninety Days With One Virtual Assistant: Every Check-In, On Camera

Most testimonials are a snapshot. Someone is happy on the day they were asked, and you are left to guess whether that lasted or what it looked like getting there.

Ken and Montse Foster run a real estate business at Keller Williams Memorial — nineteen years together, twenty-two years combined in the industry. They hired a VRA virtual assistant named Jerome, and they sat for all four check-ins on camera: day 14, day 30, day 60 and day 90. Same client, same assistant, ninety days, filmed as it happened.

This page is that sequence. It is the clearest answer we can give to the question we get asked most, which is not what a virtual assistant does on a good day but what the relationship actually looks like over time.

Day 14
“A+”

Two weeks in, Michael asked Ken to rate his assistant. The answer was one character long.

“He's doing all my calendaring. He's keeping me on track. He's going through my emails, calling me, WhatsApp-ing me, making sure I know — when I'm on the road driving all the time — what's important in my emails and what I need to take action on.”

Then the detail that says more than the grade:

“If I don't make a phone call to somebody during the day, he makes sure it's in front of me first thing the next morning that I still need to make that phone call.”

Most people describe an assistant as something that takes work away. Ken is describing the opposite: someone who hands work back, on purpose, at the moment it can actually be done. That is not what day 14 usually looks like, and it is worth noticing that it was possible this early because the assistant arrived trained rather than needing to be taught the job first.

“He's doing exactly what I asked him to do, which is being very proactive in devising systems to get me in front of more people, to make more money.”

Day 30
From one level to the next

By day 30 the work had moved from triage to output. Ken's summary was short, and the useful part of it was a gesture the camera caught better than any transcript: his assistant had taken the marketing effort from one level to a visibly higher one, in a very short period of time.

This is the step most delegation never reaches. The first month of any assistant relationship is absorbing what already exists. The second is when it starts producing something that was not happening before.

Day 60
Still getting better

At sixty days, Ken said something no other VRA client has said on camera. He was not talking about what his assistant could do. He was talking about what VRA keeps doing to him.

“Jerome got to a fast start at the beginning and he continues to improve, develop, learn. You guys actually have — and I've told Jerome this — you guys actually have great training programs that keep him in development — stuff like the mindset training that you guys currently had. So he continues to develop and move forward.”
“So, can't say enough good about Jerome, but love how you guys keep developing them, too, so that they're moving forward.”

Training before placement is the part every virtual assistant company advertises. Training after placement is the part that decides whether month eight looks like month two. Ken is a client, sixty days in, volunteering that the second kind is happening.

Day 90
“That next level”

The ninety-day check-in was Montse's. By then the question had stopped being whether the assistant was working out.

“Well, first of all, I want to thank you, because Jerome has been one of the most wonderful additions to our team. I am surprised that we didn't take this step earlier. He's incredibly well-trained, professional, has a lot of great energy, and we finally — after 19 years together, actually 22 years combined in this business — we finally feel like our business is going to that next level.”

Twenty-two years in, and the thing that moved the business was an assistant. Montse's closing remark was not about Jerome at all:

“So I couldn't thank you enough for, you know, just really great training — what you do to make sure that they come on board with our team and deliver excellent service and support, which is huge for us.”

What ninety days actually looks like

Read the four together and the shape is clear, and it is not the shape most people expect.

Nothing dramatic happened at day 14. What happened was triage — calendar, inbox, the phone call that would otherwise have been forgotten. By day 30 that foundation was producing marketing that had not been going out before. By day 60 the assistant was still improving, because the training had not stopped when the placement started. By day 90 two people with twenty-two years in the business were describing a step change.

That progression is why VRA checks in at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days rather than once at the start. Each conversation is where the scope widens on purpose. Left alone, most assistant relationships settle at whatever they were doing in week two.

It is also worth saying what the Fosters did not have to do. They did not write a job description, they did not spend their first month training someone, and they did not have to know on day one what the work would become. The assistant arrived able to do the job, and the job grew.
