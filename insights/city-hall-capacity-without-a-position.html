Adding Capacity at City Hall Without Adding a Position

The need is real and the headcount is not coming. That is the position most small cities are actually in, and it is worth naming plainly, because the usual response — absorbing the work into existing staff — is a decision nobody consciously makes.

Why Does Adding a Position Take So Long?

Because it is a budget decision before it is a staffing decision.

A new position generally needs a funding line in an adopted budget, a job description and classification, a posting period, interviews, and in many cities an action by council. Run smoothly, that is months. More to the point, it starts when the next budget cycle starts rather than when the work appears.

The work does not wait for that. It gets absorbed by whoever has the least full week, which is almost never the person best suited to it, and frequently that is where it stays. The grant calendar lapses, the outreach log goes quiet, the monthly report gets written the night before — not because anyone decided those things were unimportant, but because nobody ever decided they were somebody’s job.

What Does a Contracted Assistant Change?

Two things: the procurement path and the ability to resize.

A contracted assistant is bought as a vendor service against an existing line rather than created as a position. There is no classification to write, no posting period and no headcount authorization. The timeline becomes a procurement timeline rather than a budget-cycle timeline.

It can also be sized to the work. Part-time while the workload is uncertain, full-time once it is not, ended when a program ends. A position is difficult to unwind and everybody knows it, which is part of why creating one is treated so seriously. A service carries none of that weight, which makes it a reasonable thing to try before committing to a permanent role — and a reasonable way to find out what a permanent role would actually need to do.

What Work Can Actually Be Handed Over?

The research, tracking, drafting and coordination layer — which is most of the hours and very few of the decisions.

  • Grant research, eligibility screening and calendar upkeep
  • Business database and outreach list maintenance, and visit logging
  • Document assembly ahead of applications and submissions
  • Newsletters, website updates and social content, prepared for approval
  • Files, records, correspondence and documentation
  • Receipt tracking, accounts payable and reimbursement coordination
  • Meeting scheduling, transcription and action item tracking
  • Project trackers and the monthly report

What stays with city staff is anything carrying authority: the decisions, the approvals, the signature, and the public record of both. An assistant prepares the newsletter; the city approves what the city says. An assistant assembles the application; the city decides whether to apply.

How Does It Fit a Grant-Funded Program?

A contracted service can be scoped to a defined term, which fits a program with a funding period better than a permanent position does. The engagement can be written to the term rather than to a permanent line, and ended when the program ends without an unwinding problem.

Whether a particular cost is allowable under a particular award is a question for the city’s finance staff and the award terms, and it is worth asking early rather than at reporting time. What we can do is write the engagement to whatever term and scope the city needs.

What It Costs, and How It Is Bought

A flat $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant at 40 hours a week, or $800 a month part-time at 20 hours. No setup fee, no onboarding fee, no overage and no upcharge for specialization. No benefits load, because it is a contracted service rather than an employee.

It is procured as any vendor service is. VRA has been through a municipal approval process already and can supply what purchasing or legal review asks for: a signed confidentiality agreement, the city’s own form of agreement where one is required, confirmation of insurance coverage, and the name of the individual assigned to the office.
