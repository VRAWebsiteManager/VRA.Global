Case Study

One Market Center, Ten Virtual Assistants

Most market center leaders think of a virtual assistant as a single hire — someone to take a few tasks off one person's plate. Keller Williams Memorial in Houston took a different approach. Over time, the market center has built out an entire back office using dedicated Virtual Realty Assistants (VRA) — not one generalist, but a team of specialists, each owning a distinct function most offices either handle in-house at high cost or don't handle consistently at all.

The result is a working example of how far delegation can go when a market center stops thinking task-by-task and starts thinking function-by-function.

“He handles all of our CDAs — this month we've had over 400 — and he reaches out to 60 to 75-plus people a month for accounts receivable. It's been great to have that leverage.”
— Victoria Williams, Market Center Administrator, Keller Williams Memorial

Marketing & Content, End to End

Rather than one person juggling every marketing request, KW Memorial's VAs split the work by specialty. One handles the steady drumbeat of social content — agent birthdays and anniversaries, recognition posts for Fan Four, Cappers, Club Elite, and Top 1% producers, new associate announcements, and class and event promotion — plus on-demand flyers, email marketing, and social engagement. A second VA works directly with individual agents to build their personal brand: flyers, postcards, brochures, business cards, signage, and listing presentations, customized to each agent's preferences.

A third role goes further than most market centers attempt at all: a dedicated Video Content Specialist who runs the office's Video Content Program from end to end — conducting consultations with agents, writing and revising scripts, filming the shoots, editing the final content, and managing every file along the way. Video production is one of the highest-friction marketing tasks for most agents to do themselves; having it run as a standing service is a genuine differentiator.

Recruiting & Agent Attraction

Growth doesn't run on marketing alone. A dedicated Campaign Coordinator manages the market center's recruiting pipeline — tracking scheduled recruits, maintaining the recruiting spreadsheet and CRM records, and sending daily outreach to targeted prospects. The same coordinator manages the Luxury division's weekly Mailchimp campaigns, Luxury open house listings, and a monthly market report ranking the top 10 agents by market area. On the executive side, recruiting appointments are scheduled and supported directly, with weekly agent-in/agent-out reports and monthly recruiting reports prepared for accounting — so leadership always has a current picture of pipeline health without pulling the data themselves.

Accounting Support, Compliance & Agent Services

This is the function Victoria Williams' quote above describes, and the data list behind it is extensive: entering and uploading Commission Disbursement Authorizations (CDAs) in Command, cross-checking commission splits and mentor fees ahead of MCA review, processing revisions, monthly office bill invoicing, agent billing in AccountEdge, and monthly production reports for the Million Dollar Club and Fantastic Four — over 400 CDAs a month at KW Memorial's volume. A separate Agent Services role handles the full onboarding and offboarding lifecycle across a dozen different systems (AgentsOnly, Wix, BrokerKit, BrokerBot, Agent Forum, and more), new-agent welcome graphics, orientation reminders, and Preferred Partner Program management — the kind of high-volume, detail-heavy administrative work that quietly consumes a market center's staff time if it isn't owned by someone specifically.

Command & Tech Support

New and existing agents need ongoing help with the technology stack they're required to use, and KW Memorial routes that through dedicated VA support: one-on-one tech orientations covering login setup, DISC assessments, KW Command training, e-signature setup, Dotloop and Lone Wolf transactions, and required MLS classes, plus ongoing troubleshooting and inbound call support for agents who run into issues later. It's a role that directly reduces how often agents need to escalate technical problems to market center staff.

Beyond Residential: Commercial and Luxury

KW Memorial's commercial division runs its own dedicated support — transaction coordination for both commercial real estate and business brokerage deals, monthly PIE meeting logistics and presentations, listing flyers, and skip tracing and CMA support on request. Combined with the Luxury division's campaign management described above, it shows that specialized VA support isn't limited to standard residential transactions — it scales into the more complex, higher-touch parts of the business too.

Executive-Level Support for Leadership

The same model extends to the top of the market center. Catherine, Executive Assistant to President and Team Leader Roger Aad, has become exactly the kind of extension of leadership Roger described in his own review of VRA:

“Catherine has become my right hand… she isn't simply completing tasks. She is proactive, creative, and constantly thinking ahead.”
— Roger Aad, President and Team Leader, Keller Williams Memorial

That support spans calendar and communications management, recruiting appointment coordination and reporting, class and guest-speaker logistics, and document coordination for leadership review and signature — the layer of executive support that lets an operating principal or team leader stay focused on agents and growth instead of their own inbox.

Leadership support at KW Memorial doesn't stop at the team leader's desk. Brandon Lara, an Assistant Team Leader at the market center, described what the first 90 days with a dedicated assistant actually looked like — and what stood out to him wasn't task volume, it was preparation:

“I think one of my favorite parts about having an assistant, especially through VRA, is because she knew the tools before we even started. And on top of that, she’s thinking one step ahead of me, because she read my KPA. She understands my DISC. So she knows exactly where my pain points are, and she’s working on those first so that I can focus on where my wins are.”
— Brandon Lara, Assistant Team Leader, Keller Williams Memorial (watch the 90-day check-in)

That detail matters more at the leadership level than anywhere else in a market center. An assistant who arrives already fluent in Command and Dotloop, and who has read the leader's own KPA and DISC profile, starts contributing in week one instead of spending a quarter learning the environment.

What This Means for Other Market Centers

KW Memorial didn't build this team overnight, and no market center needs to replicate all ten roles on day one. What the case study actually demonstrates is the ceiling: marketing, recruiting, accounting support, tech support, commercial transactions, and executive operations can all run through trained, dedicated virtual assistants — not as a one-off experiment, but as standing operational infrastructure. For most market center leaders, the open question isn't whether a VA could help. It's which of these functions is already quietly eating the most time on their own plate.
