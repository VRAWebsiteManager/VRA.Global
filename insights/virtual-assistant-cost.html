How Much Does a Virtual Assistant Cost? And Why That Is So Hard to Find Out

How much does a virtual assistant cost? It is a simple question, and you can spend an afternoon failing to answer it. The published guides put virtual assistant pricing somewhere between $4 and $79 an hour. Monthly figures run from $500 to $5,000 and up. Those are not competing estimates of the same thing; they are the same industry describing itself badly.

VRA charges $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant and $800 a month for part-time. That is the whole answer, and it is on our pricing page where you can read it without talking to anyone. The rest of this article is about why that is unusual, and what to look for when a price is harder to pin down.

Four Ways the Price Gets Hidden

The number is rarely withheld outright. It is more often obscured in one of four ways, and each one shifts work onto you.

1. No price at all. Some providers publish nothing. Software review sites note the gap plainly — one major directory states that a vendor’s pricing and plan availability are simply not listed, and that buyers typically need to engage the vendor directly to find out. That is a sales call standing between you and a budget number.

2. A starting-at figure. “Starting at $1,988 a month” is a floor, not a price. It tells you what the cheapest possible version costs for the simplest possible need, which is almost never the thing you are buying. You still cannot budget, and the real figure arrives after a strategy call.

3. Published but inconsistent. Some providers do publish, then contradict themselves. We read one widely-ranked pricing guide that quotes $4 to $79 an hour in its body copy and $7 to $65 an hour in its own FAQ, with two different monthly ranges in between. Transparent in form, unusable in practice.

4. Tiers you cannot place yourself in. This is the one that catches people. A provider lists “managed” support at one rate and “specialized” support $500 a month higher, without saying which one your work is. Another sells “basic” against “elite” at a $1,000 monthly gap and never defines elite. You are being asked to diagnose your own requirements, in someone else’s vocabulary, before you are allowed to know the price — and if you guess low, you find out in month two.

What Genuinely Drives the Hourly Rate

Some of the spread is real. Four things actually drive virtual assistant cost, and they are worth knowing before you compare any two quotes.

Where the assistant is. This is the largest single factor and it accounts for most of the $4-to-$79 spread. A US-based assistant and an offshore one are different products at different prices.

Dedicated or pooled. A dedicated assistant works your hours and learns your business. A pooled model draws from a shared team, so the person handling your work on Thursday may not be the one who handled it on Monday. Pooled is cheaper per hour and more expensive in rework.

Agency or marketplace. A marketplace is a lower rate and you do the screening, training, management and replacement yourself. An agency rate includes that work. Comparing the two on hourly rate alone compares a part to a whole.

Specialization upcharges. Many providers price industry knowledge as an add-on. This is where the tier problem above turns into real money.

How to Compare Any Two Quotes

Four questions will usually expose whether a number is the real one.

  • Is this the price, or the starting price?
  • Is the assistant dedicated to us, or shared across clients?
  • Does the rate change if our work turns out to need industry knowledge?
  • What happens, and what does it cost, if the person is not the right fit?

A provider who can answer all four in writing, before a call, is quoting you a price. A provider who needs a call first is quoting you a conversation.

Virtual Assistant Cost: Common Questions

How much does a virtual assistant cost?

Published rates across the industry run from about $4 to $79 an hour, which is too wide to budget against. The spread is driven mostly by where the assistant is based, and by whether they are dedicated to you or shared across several clients. VRA charges a flat $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant at 40 hours a week, or $800 a month for part-time at 20 hours. That works out to roughly $8.70 an hour full-time.

What is the average hourly rate for a virtual assistant?

There is no single average worth quoting, which is the honest answer. Published guides put US-based assistants around $25 to $45 an hour and offshore assistants around $4 to $20, and the same guides often contradict themselves between their body copy and their own FAQs. Compare the full monthly figure for a defined number of hours instead of the hourly rate on its own.

Why does virtual assistant pricing vary so much?

Four things explain most of it: where the assistant is located, whether they are dedicated or drawn from a shared pool, whether you are buying from an agency or a marketplace where you do the screening and management yourself, and whether the provider charges extra for industry specialization. Two quotes can differ threefold and both be accurate for what they describe.

Do virtual assistant rates change depending on the industry?

At many providers, yes. Industry knowledge is commonly priced as a tier or an upcharge, which is why you are often asked to place yourself in a category before you are quoted. At VRA the rate is the same whether your assistant supports a title office, a mortgage team, a real estate brokerage, a restaurant group or a home services company. There are no tiers and no specialization surcharge.

What should a virtual assistant’s monthly cost include?

Ask what happens around the hours, not just the hours. A rate should cover matching and onboarding, ongoing support after the start date, and a remedy if the person is not the right fit. VRA includes hands-on matching, check-ins at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, and the ability to swap to a different assistant at any point at no extra cost. Every assistant completes a four-week instructor-led training programme before touching client work.

The price is $1,500 a month full-time, $800 part-time. No call required to find that out.
