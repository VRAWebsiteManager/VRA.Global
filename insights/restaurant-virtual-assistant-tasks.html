Virtual Assistant for Restaurants and Chefs: What to Hand Off, and What to Keep

The short answer: a restaurant virtual assistant takes on the desk work around service: reservations and confirmations, phone and message coverage, vendor follow-up and ordering, menu and price updates across delivery apps and your website, review responses, and invoicing. The floor, the line, the recipes and the decisions stay with you and your team. This guide shows how to find the admin hours in your own week, which ones to hand off first, how to hand them off without losing control, and what changes if you’re buying or selling a restaurant.

Where Do a Restaurant Owner’s Admin Hours Go?

The admin in a restaurant doesn’t arrive in one block. It arrives in pieces: a vendor text between lunch and dinner, a menu change that has to go out to three delivery apps, a review that needs a reply, a large-party request sitting in the inbox, an invoice that doesn’t match the delivery. Each one takes a few minutes. Together they fill the hours you meant to spend on the kitchen, your staff or the next location.

Before you hand anything off, find out where those hours actually go in your business. For two weeks, keep a running list of every admin task you or your managers do, and every question someone brings to you that isn’t about food or service. Note roughly how long each one took and what happened when it slipped. At the end, sort the list into groups:

  • Guest-facing: reservations, confirmations, phone calls, messages, large-party and event inquiries.
  • Vendors and money: ordering, vendor follow-up, invoices, the vendor list, bookkeeping.
  • Online presence: menus on delivery apps and your website, online ordering platforms, reviews, social media.
  • People: shift communication, staff scheduling, job postings and interviews.

Start with the group that takes the biggest share of your hours, or costs you real money when it slips.

What Restaurant Admin Can You Hand Off?

Everything below is desk work: it can be written down, done remotely, and checked.

Reservations and the phone

Reservation management and confirmations, plus phone and message coverage, so a caller asking about a table, a large party or your hours gets an answer instead of voicemail. Group, event and venue inquiries can be coordinated the same way, and if events are a real line of business rather than the occasional large party, the whole booking desk is covered in virtual assistant for catering and private events. The questions answered, the details collected, the request put in front of you for a decision.

Vendors, ordering and invoices

Vendor coordination, ordering and vendor follow-up, keeping the vendor list current, invoicing and invoice tracking, and bookkeeping support in QuickBooks. What gets ordered and from whom is your call. The assistant handles the follow-up and the paperwork around it.

Menus and delivery apps

Menu, price and availability updates across delivery apps, your online ordering platform and your website. When you change a dish or a price, it goes out everywhere, and you don’t have to do it at midnight.

Reviews and your online presence

Customer review monitoring and responses, written in the voice you set, plus social media management and email marketing campaigns. If promotion matters to your restaurant, an assistant matched to restaurants can add VRA’s Social & Selling marketing certification, which covers social content creation, short-form video and brand voice.

Staff scheduling and hiring

Staff scheduling support and shift communication, and recruiting coordination: job postings, applicant tracking and interview scheduling. Who you hire, and who works which shift, stay your decisions.

Whatever else runs through your systems

Your restaurant’s admin won’t match a list exactly. Assistants are trained in CRM fundamentals that carry to whatever system your business runs on, so the desk work in your reservation platform, POS, delivery-app dashboards or back-office software can be handed off the same way. If it can be written down, done remotely and checked, it’s a candidate. And whatever you run, we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

Writing it all down

As your assistant takes on each task, the process gets written down: which vendor needs a call instead of an email, which regulars get a call back first, how a large-party request is handled. Your business keeps those documents. Our guide to SOPs for small business covers a simple one-page format.

How Do You Hand Off Restaurant Admin Without Losing Control?

Start with one group. Pick the group from your two-week list that costs you the greatest number of hours, hand that over, and add the next one once it comes back right. Handing over everything in week one makes it hard to see what’s working. If you are weighing an assistant against a first payroll hire, we set the two side by side.

Write down the exceptions first. The regular who always gets the corner table, the supplier who only answers by phone, the menu item that’s off the delivery apps on Sundays. Those are the details only you know, and the details that make the work come back right.

Set the limits in writing. Decide what your assistant can answer without asking you (hours, booking a standard table, confirming an order), what needs your sign-off (a large-party quote, a public reply to a one-star review, a change to a standing order), and how fast you’ll respond when they ask.

Grant access by role, not by password. Give your assistant their own logins to the reservation system, delivery-app dashboards and inbox where you can, scoped to the work, so you can revoke access at any time.

Agree on a daily recap. A short end-of-day note covering what came in, what got done and what’s waiting on you keeps you informed without checking every message yourself.

A Person, an AI Phone Tool, or Both?

Automated phone and chat tools can answer the questions you’ve scripted in advance. The work around reservations, vendors and reviews calls for judgment in the moment: the caller who wants to move a party of twelve, the vendor follow-up that needs a real conversation, the review that needs a reply in your voice. VRA’s position is simple: AI is a tool our assistants use under your direction, not a substitute for the person you hired. If you already use an automated tool, an assistant can work alongside it and pick up what it can’t handle. The software VRA assistants work in is listed on tools and platforms.

Buying or Selling a Restaurant

A restaurant changing hands is a moment when the admin is exposed. The vendor relationships, the reservation habits and the ordering rhythm can live in the seller’s head and leave when they do.

If you’re selling, you can engage a VRA assistant before closing to administer the back office and write down how it works while the restaurant is still yours. At closing, the engagement can transfer to the buyer, so the person who knows the vendors and the systems stays with the business. Who pays, and from when, is negotiated by you and the buyer. Whether you hold handover calls with the buyer and the assistant is optional and up to the two of you. VRA’s 3-month minimum starts with the seller and carries over to the buyer at closing, and terms can be adjusted to fit the deal.
