Fifteen Virtual Assistants, Four Functions, No Real Estate in Sight

Most companies hire a virtual assistant the way they’d hire a temp: one person, a few tasks, see how it goes. EIDLexit went the other way. The nationwide services firm — which helps small business owners resolve SBA disaster loan debt, close businesses down cleanly, and start over — now runs its sales team, its client service team, and dedicated marketing and HR support on Virtual Realty Assistants (VRA) virtual assistants. Fifteen of them.

It’s worth saying plainly what this case study is not: it has nothing to do with real estate. EIDLexit doesn’t list houses or close title files. That’s exactly why it matters.

“Exceeding expectations, for sure. Very excited about the team that we’ve been able to build through you guys — it’s been an amazing partnership up to this point.”
— Quinton Randel, Co-Founder & EVP, Operations and Technology, EIDLexit

Four Functions, Not Fifteen Odd Jobs

The reason fifteen assistants work as a team rather than a crowd is that the roles are split by function, not by task list. EIDLexit is a consultation-driven business: prospects book a free consultation, and clients then move through a document-heavy process. Those are very different jobs, so VRA staffs them separately — and as the company grew, two more functions followed.

On the sales side, VAs work the front of the funnel — outbound calling from lead lists, fast follow-up on inbound inquiries, booking consultations onto the calendar, keeping CRM records clean and current, and chasing the no-shows and not-yets that most teams let go cold.

On the client service side, VAs carry the work after the sale — onboarding new clients, collecting and chasing documents, tracking case status, keeping clients updated before they have to ask, and preparing organized packages for review by the company’s own licensed professionals.

Two more roles sit outside those benches entirely. A dedicated marketing VA owns the company’s marketing work, and an HR-focused VA supports the people side of a growing team. Both are single seats doing specialist work — the kind of hire a company this size usually postpones for years because one person isn’t worth a full domestic salary yet.

The Bar Is Client-Facing Communication

Plenty of businesses will let a virtual assistant do back-office work. Far fewer will let one talk to their clients. That’s the real test, and it’s where Quinton is most direct:

“Very high professionalism, both with the internal communication and the way that they operate and carry themselves, but also with clients… They know how to communicate at a very high professional level.”
— Quinton Randel, EIDLexit

That trust isn’t automatic, and it isn’t instant. It follows training — both VRA’s mandatory 4-week program and the client’s own standard operating procedures. Quinton describes it as a function of both: once the assistants are trained and they’ve learned how EIDLexit works and how the company likes to communicate, they operate as part of the team.

Why a Bench Beats a Hire

Building the same capability in-house means fifteen job postings, fifteen interviews, payroll, benefits, equipment, and the risk that several of them don’t work out. With VRA, each seat is a flat monthly rate, each assistant arrives already trained in professional communication, CRM and database management, document preparation, and AI-assisted workflows, and an assistant who isn’t the right fit can be swapped at any point at no extra cost.

It also scales in the direction most businesses actually need: one seat at a time. EIDLexit didn’t start with fifteen — at the time the video above was filmed, the team was ten.

What Travels to Your Business

You don’t have to be in loan resolution for this to apply. The pattern fits any business that sells through consultations, serves clients through a process, and eventually needs specialist support it can’t yet justify hiring for:

  • Consulting and professional services firms booking discovery calls and onboarding new clients
  • Insurance, financial, and advisory practices with document-heavy intake
  • Agencies and B2B service companies that need consistent follow-up and account support
  • Any team where leads go cold overnight or clients have to chase you for a status update
  • Growing companies that need marketing or HR support before a full-time hire makes sense

How this works: VRA virtual assistants provide administrative, sales, and customer support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal, financial, or other advice requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by the client and are subject to the client’s own security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.

Start With One

Fifteen assistants is where EIDLexit is now, not where they began. The practical move is to pick the single function that’s costing you the most — the leads nobody called back, or the clients waiting on an update — and staff that one seat first.
