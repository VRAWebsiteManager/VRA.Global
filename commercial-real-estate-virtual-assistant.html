Why This Page Exists

Commercial Is Not Residential With Bigger Numbers

In virtual assistance, “real estate” usually means residential. The deal cycle is different, the document load is different, and follow-up that stops after three weeks is follow-up that was never going to close anything.

What makes commercial harder to staff

  • A file that stays open for months, not weeks, and has to stay organized the whole time
  • More documents per deal, and more parties who each need a different subset of them
  • Follow-up that has to continue long after a residential deal would have closed or died
  • Research — comparables, ownership, public records — that takes hours and has to be current
  • Several live listings at once, each at a different stage

What that means for the assistant

  • Document control is the first job, before anything else is possible
  • Deadlines are tracked in a system, not remembered
  • Follow-up runs on a cadence the assistant owns, not on whether you got to it
  • The assistant has to be trusted in front of clients and brokers, not kept behind you
  • Scope widens as the book is learned — it does not start wide

FAQ

Questions Commercial Teams Ask

How a VRA virtual assistant works inside a commercial or business brokerage practice — the scope, the ramp, the hours, and the cost.

What does a commercial real estate virtual assistant do?

A VRA commercial assistant handles the administrative side of a commercial or business brokerage practice: organizing deal documents in your system, keeping listing files current, running follow-up on active listings so the pipeline keeps moving, property and market research, marketing and social support, administrative underwriting support, and transaction coordination.

Is commercial different from residential for a virtual assistant?

The deal cycle is longer, there are more documents per file, and follow-up runs over months rather than weeks. The administrative work itself — document control, deadline tracking, research, follow-up — is the same discipline. VRA places assistants with commercial brokers, business brokerage teams and commercial divisions, and matches on the work rather than on the asset class.

Can an assistant support underwriting and property research?

Administratively, yes — assembling the inputs, pulling comparables and public records, building and maintaining the file, and keeping models and trackers up to date with numbers you supply.

Can one assistant cover both commercial and residential work?

Yes. Several VRA clients run mixed books, and the assistant works across both. One commercial assistant at VRA currently supports two separate commercial clients in different parts of the same brokerage.

How long before a commercial assistant is useful?

It builds. The common pattern across VRA's commercial clients is organization first — documents, files and systems brought under control in the first month — then listing and pipeline follow-up as the assistant learns the book. VRA checks in at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days so that progression is deliberate rather than left to chance.

What does a commercial real estate virtual assistant cost?

$1,500 per month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week) or $800 per month for a part-time assistant (20 hours a week). Flat rate, no hidden fees and no tiered upcharge for specialization, subject to applicable agreement terms.

How are commercial real estate virtual assistants trained and screened?

Every assistant completes a mandatory 4-week, instructor-led training program before touching client work. Exceptional English communication skills are a requirement to be hired at VRA. Every assistant comes through screening, hands-on training and a final evaluation — in our most recent cohort, 1,800 applications produced 16 graduating assistants, or 0.89%. VRA deliberately admits more people into training than will pass, because the filter is hands-on performance and a final evaluation rather than a résumé.

Can a virtual assistant work in CoStar and LoopNet?

Yes, both. Assistants working with our commercial clients pull property and market data in CoStar and keep listings current in LoopNet — posting and updating details, photos, documents and status, so what is live matches what you are actually marketing. The same assistants work in KW Command, RealNex and DocuSign or Authentisign. Your assistant works in your own accounts, at the permission level you set.

What should a commercial team delegate first?

Organization, then the follow-up that runs longest. Both commercial clients whose check-ins are on camera describe the same first month: document control, file naming, where things live. An assistant working inside a disorganized system spends their time asking where things are. Once that is settled, the priority is the follow-up on listings in flight, because commercial pipelines stall quietly months after the work that started them, and those touches are the first thing to slip when a broker is working the next deal.
