Your Listings Shouldn’t Depend on the Owner

You know the file. Good revenue, loyal customers, clean enough books — and an owner who is the only person alive who knows how any of it actually works. Buyers price that in. Lenders ask about it. Some of them walk. A trained VRA assistant documents the procedures and administers the back office, so the business can show it keeps moving when the owner steps away from it.

The Problem You Already Know

The Discount Isn’t About the Numbers

Owner dependency is the quietest thing on the file and the most expensive. It does not show up as an objection. It shows up as a lower offer, a longer close, or a buyer who stops returning calls. For sellers who want to work on it: how to make a business run without its owner.

Where the value leaks out

  • Pricing, scheduling and exceptions live in the owner’s head
  • Vendor and customer relationships are personal, not institutional
  • Nothing is written down, so nothing can be handed over
  • The owner cannot take two weeks off, let alone leave permanently
  • Training the buyer means a transition period nobody wants to fund

What the buyer is really pricing

  • The risk that revenue follows the seller out the door
  • Months of their own time spent learning an undocumented job
  • A hire they’ll have to make immediately, at full market cost
  • The chance that the one employee who knows things leaves too
  • Everything they can’t verify because it was never recorded
Before the Listing Goes Live

Turn the Owner’s Head Into a Written Back Office

VRA trains assistants specifically to identify how a business actually operates and write it down. That work was built for ownership transitions — it is not a byproduct of general admin support.

1

Document the procedures

The assistant works alongside the owner and writes the SOPs as they go — intake, scheduling, billing, vendors, the exceptions that never made it into a manual.

2

Take over the administration

Phones, inbox, calendar, invoicing, CRM, follow-up. The owner stops being the one doing it, which is the only honest test of whether it was ever documented properly.

3

Show that it holds

By the time the listing is live, the business has been operating with the owner one step back — and there is a record of it rather than an assurance.

4

Hand over documents, not folklore

What transfers at close is a written back office and someone already administering it, instead of two weeks of the seller trying to remember everything.

How the engagement is structured

Seller Starts, Buyer Keeps: One Assistant Across the Close

Your seller can engage an assistant before listing, to document the procedures and administer the back office while the business is on the market. At closing, the engagement can transfer to the buyer, so the buyer inherits a written back office and the person already administering it, not a stranger. Who pays, and from when, is a term the buyer and seller set in their deal, in the purchase agreement or transition plan, alongside everything else on the table. VRA is flexible on the process. If an assistant ever has to be replaced, the next one works from the same procedures, because those belong to the business.

The buyer does not inherit a stranger. They inherit the one person who has been doing the day-to-day work and wrote down how it is done. For what the buyer faces after the keys change hands, see your first 100 days after buying a business.

Questions Brokers Ask

Before You Put Your Name On It

Can the assistant transfer to the buyer at closing?

Yes. The engagement can transfer to the buyer at closing, so the same assistant continues with the new owner. VRA’s only commitment is a 3-month minimum. It starts with the seller and carries over to the buyer at closing, and terms can be adjusted to fit the deal. Who pays, and from when, is set by your client and the buyer in their deal, for example in the purchase agreement or transition plan. The procedures the assistant documents belong to the business, so they transfer with it either way.

Is this a temporary engagement?

No. A seller engaging an assistant before listing is starting a placement that can continue with the new owner after close, not hiring a contractor for the duration of the sale. The aim is for the buyer to keep the assistant because they’re worth keeping, not because of a contract.

What about confidentiality while the business is on the market?

Every VRA assistant signs a confidentiality agreement, and VRA will sign your client’s NDA on request. Assistants work inside the systems the business already uses, under the access the owner grants them.

What if the buyer wants to make their own hire instead?

Then they inherit written procedures either way, which is the part that holds the value. The documentation belongs to the business, not to us.

Does this only work for certain industries?

No. VRA assistants are trained as generalists and matched to the business, and the procedure-documentation work is the same whether it is a restaurant, a contractor, a professional practice or a distributor.

Who manages the assistant day to day?

The owner does. Assistants carry out and take ownership of the processes they are given; they administer the back office rather than supervise anyone. VRA handles matching, onboarding and ongoing support behind them.

Is there a referral arrangement?

Yes. VRA pays a referral fee when an introduction becomes a placement. Michael will walk you through the terms on a call — and the arrangement is there whether or not you want it to be part of the conversation with your client.

How involved do I have to be?

As involved as you want. You can make an introduction and step back.
