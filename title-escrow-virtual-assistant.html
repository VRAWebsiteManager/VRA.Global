FAQ

Questions Title Teams Ask

How a VRA virtual assistant works inside a title or escrow operation — the scope, the systems, the hours, and the cost.

What does a title and escrow virtual assistant do?

A VRA title and escrow virtual assistant handles the repeatable file work inside your production system: importing title commitments from Marketplace, hyperlinking commitments for reference, pre-closing file review and checklist support across commitments, buyer, seller and lender information, tax certificates, tax statements and payoff statements, administrative updates to Schedule C after the required entity documents have been reviewed and approved by the appropriate title professional or underwriter, and coordinating documents for underwriter and lender review. Some title clients also use their assistant for marketing and social media management.

Can a VRA virtual assistant work inside Qualia?

Yes. Qualia is the production system VRA trains title and escrow assistants in before they start with a client. Your assistant works inside the system your office already runs, with the access you provision and can change or revoke at any time.

What if our office runs SoftPro or another title production system?

Qualia is the system VRA trains title assistants in, not SoftPro. What we commit to is the ramp: the closing workflow is the same in any title production system, so an assistant trained in one picks up another quickly — and if your office runs SoftPro or another platform, we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

What stays with our licensed staff?

Judgment and licensed work. VRA virtual assistants provide administrative and operational support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal advice, underwriting decisions, escrow authority, or services requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by you and are subject to your office’s security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.

Is the assistant assigned only to us?

You are matched with one named assistant who learns your files and your process, rather than a rotating pool. A full-time assistant works for your business and no one else; a part-time assistant is your own named assistant with reserved hours. Matching starts with a two-minute Needs Assessment, and every assistant is screened with a DISC Assessment and a Keller Personality Assessment for fit and fast-learning ability.

How are VRA assistants trained and screened for title work?

Every assistant completes a mandatory 4-week, instructor-led training program before touching client work, and assistants headed for title and escrow teams are trained in Qualia. Assistants supporting title teams are also trained on the contract documents, timelines and file-review procedures a closing file runs on, with state-specific contract training for Texas and Florida. Exceptional English communication skills are a requirement to be hired at VRA. Every assistant comes through screening, hands-on training and a final evaluation — in our most recent cohort, 1,800 applications produced 16 graduating assistants, or 0.89%. VRA deliberately admits more people into training than will pass, because the filter is hands-on performance and a final evaluation rather than a résumé.

What does a title and escrow virtual assistant cost?

$1,500 per month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week) or $800 per month for a part-time assistant (20 hours a week). Flat rate, no hidden fees and no tiered upcharge for industry specialization, subject to applicable agreement terms.

Why do you publish your pricing when many agencies won’t quote until after a discovery call?

Because you should be able to decide whether this fits your budget before you spend time on a call. VRA charges $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant and $800 a month for part-time, flat, with no tiered upcharge for industry specialization and no hidden fees, subject to applicable agreement terms. The rate is the same whether your assistant works in title and escrow, real estate, or hospitality.

Can your assistant run a title search or do curative work?

They can order a title search and track what comes back, and they can do curative document follow-up — gathering the paperwork your examiner has asked for and chasing the parties who owe it. What they do not do is examine title, decide whether a defect is cleared, or make any underwriting call. Those stay with your licensed staff.

When should a title office hire help rather than absorb the work?

When files start closing late for administrative reasons rather than title reasons. If documents are sitting because nobody had an hour, or client updates are going out a day behind, the admin has outgrown the team. That usually arrives well before the volume justifies another salary, which is what part-time at $800 a month for 20 hours a week is for.
