FAQ

Questions Title Teams Ask

How a VRA virtual assistant works inside a title or escrow operation — the scope, the systems, the hours, and the cost.

What does a title and escrow virtual assistant do?

A VRA title and escrow virtual assistant handles the repeatable file work inside your production system: importing title commitments from Marketplace, hyperlinking commitments for reference, pre-closing file review and checklist support across commitments, buyer, seller and lender information, tax certificates, tax statements and payoff statements, administrative updates to Schedule C after the required entity documents have been reviewed and approved by the appropriate title professional or underwriter, and coordinating documents for underwriter and lender review. Some title clients also use their assistant for marketing and social media management.

Can a VRA virtual assistant work inside Qualia?

Yes. Qualia is the production system VRA trains title and escrow assistants in before they start with a client. Your assistant works inside the system your office already runs, with the access you provision and can change or revoke at any time.

What if our office runs SoftPro or another title production system?

Qualia is the system VRA trains title assistants in, not SoftPro. What we guarantee is the ramp: the closing workflow is the same in any title production system, so an assistant trained in one picks up another quickly — and if your office runs SoftPro or another platform, your assistant reaches 90% proficiency in it within 5 business days.

What stays with our licensed staff?

Judgment and licensed work. VRA virtual assistants provide administrative and operational support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal advice, underwriting decisions, escrow authority, or services requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by you and are subject to your office’s security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.

Is the assistant dedicated to our office?

Yes. You are matched with one assistant who learns your files and your process, rather than a rotating pool. Matching starts with a two-minute Needs Assessment, and every assistant is screened with a DISC Assessment and a Keller Personality Assessment for fit and fast-learning ability.

How quickly can an assistant start?

Matching typically takes about a week to put candidates in front of you. Once you select your assistant, they can realistically start within 24 hours.

What hours do VRA assistants work?

U.S. business hours, Monday through Friday, so there is no time zone gap to manage. You set the schedule within that: 40 hours a week for a full-time assistant, 20 hours a week for part-time.

Can we get a bilingual assistant, and does it cost more?

Yes, and no. VRA places Spanish–English bilingual assistants for offices with Spanish-speaking buyers, sellers and agents, at the same flat rate as any other assistant. There is no upcharge for bilingual or specialized talent.

How are VRA assistants trained and screened for title work?

Every assistant completes a mandatory 4-week, instructor-led training program before touching client work, and assistants headed for title and escrow teams are trained in Qualia. Assistants supporting Texas title clients also receive training on common Texas real estate contract documents, timelines, and file-review procedures relevant to their administrative role. Exceptional English communication skills are a requirement to be hired at VRA. Only the top 1% of applicants make it through screening, hands-on training, and a final evaluation — in our most recent cohort, 1,800 applications produced 16 graduating assistants, or 0.89%. VRA deliberately admits more people into training than will pass, because the filter is hands-on performance and a final evaluation rather than a résumé.

What does a title and escrow virtual assistant cost?

$1,500 per month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week, 1-year agreement) or $800 per month for a part-time assistant (20 hours a week, 6-month agreement). Flat rate, no hidden fees and no tiered upcharge for industry specialization, subject to applicable agreement terms.

Why do you publish your pricing when many agencies won’t quote until after a discovery call?

Because you should be able to decide whether this fits your budget before you spend time on a call. VRA charges $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant and $800 a month for part-time, flat, with no tiered upcharge for industry specialization and no hidden fees, subject to applicable agreement terms. The rate is the same whether your assistant works in title and escrow, real estate, or hospitality.

What if we need a skill we didn’t anticipate?

That is what the check-ins are for. If a 14, 30, 60 or 90-day check-in surfaces a skill your office needs, VRA gives your assistant access to the matching course and trains them in it — so you keep the person who already knows your files instead of starting over with someone new.

What if the assistant isn’t the right fit?

VRA checks in with every client at 14, 30, 60, and 90 days. If the fit isn’t right, you can swap in a different assistant at any point, at no extra cost — there is no trial window to beat.

How do you protect our client data?

Every VRA assistant’s identity is verified against a government-issued photo ID on a live video call as part of onboarding, and the name on that ID is matched to their application and payment records. VRA will sign your non-disclosure agreement on request. Assistants work inside the systems you already use, on access you provision and can revoke at any time, and nothing is exported to a separate platform. VRA also carries a $1 million Errors & Omissions insurance policy.

Is VRA insured?

Yes. VRA carries a $1 million Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance policy.
