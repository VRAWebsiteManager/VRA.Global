For Owners Preparing to Sell

Preparing to Sell? Make the Business Run Without You.

It’s worth more when it doesn’t need you. You have run it for years. You know every account, every exception, every supplier who has to be called rather than emailed. That knowledge is the reason the place works — and the reason a buyer will mark it down. Before you go to market, a trained VRA assistant writes down how the business actually operates and takes over the administration, so what you are selling is a company rather than a job with worse benefits.

What You Are Actually Selling

A Buyer Is Not Paying for Last Year’s Revenue

They are paying for next year’s, and the only question that matters is whether it arrives without you in the building. Everything a buyer cannot verify, they assume the worst about and price accordingly.

What lifts the number

  • Procedures written down, in a form someone else can follow
  • Customer and supplier relationships that sit with the company
  • Administration that already happens without the owner touching it
  • A demonstrated stretch where you were not involved day to day
  • Questions in diligence that get answered with a document

What pulls it down

  • Pricing and exceptions that exist only in your memory
  • Accounts that stay because of you personally
  • An owner who would not pass the week-away test — take a week off and the business feels it
  • A handover that depends on how much you can remember out loud
  • A buyer running the numbers on what it costs to replace you

The Part Nobody Warns You About

Four Ways Depending On You Follows You Past Closing Day

The discount is the visible cost. These are the ones owners discover afterward. If you are earlier than that, start with how to make your business run without you.

1

A lower number

The most straightforward version. The buyer prices the risk that the business is really a job, and the offer reflects it.

2

Money held back

Part of the price gets tied to how the business performs after you leave — which is a strange bet to accept when the business has always run on you.

3

A longer stay

You agree to a few months of transition. It becomes a year, because nobody can answer the questions except you.

4

Carrying part of it yourself

If you are asked to finance a slice of the sale, you keep the exposure — and the new owner struggling is suddenly your problem again.

Before You Go to Market

Getting It Out of Your Head and Onto Paper

VRA trains assistants to work out how a business really operates and write it down. That training was built for exactly this moment.

What the assistant does

  • Sits alongside you and records how the work is actually done, exceptions included
  • Takes over the daily administration — phones, calendar, invoicing, follow-up
  • Keeps the admin going while you step back, which is the only real test of whether it was captured properly
  • Leaves behind written procedures — SOPs that belong to the business, not to anyone’s memory
  • Carries on with the new owner afterward, so the seller transition is a person as well as a folder

None of this requires you to decide today that you are definitely selling. It is useful the moment you start thinking about it, and it is useful if you change your mind.

Seller starts, buyer keeps

Start Before You List. Your Assistant Can Stay After You Sell.

Your assistant can start while the business is still yours: documenting how it really operates, administering the daily back office, and giving you a stretch of stepping back that a buyer can see for themselves. At closing, the engagement can transfer to the buyer, so the person who learned how everything works stays with the company instead of leaving the day you do. Who pays, and from when, is something you and your buyer set in your deal, in the purchase agreement or transition plan. The written procedures belong to the business either way. The terms are yours to negotiate with your broker and attorney; VRA provides the administrative support.

Questions Owners Ask

Before You Start

How far ahead of a sale should this start?

Sooner is better, because the value is in having actually operated with you one step back rather than in the documents alone. Assistants are placed for months rather than weeks, so that is the practical floor — but owners who start a year out get something closer to a proven result than a recent change.

Is this confidential? We are only exploring the idea.

Yes. Every VRA assistant signs a confidentiality agreement and VRA will sign yours on request. Nothing about the engagement requires anyone to know you are considering a sale — from the inside it looks like hiring administrative help, because that is what it is.

Will my staff think I am replacing them?

The assistant administers the back office; they do not supervise anybody, and they are not a manager. In practice they take work off the people who have been absorbing it, which is you and whoever has been covering for you.

What if I decide not to sell after all?

Then you own a business that runs without you. Nothing about this only pays off at a sale.

What happens to my assistant when I sell?

The engagement can transfer to the buyer at closing, so the same assistant continues with the new owner. VRA’s only commitment is a 3-month minimum. It starts with the seller and carries over to the buyer at closing, and terms can be adjusted to fit your deal. Who pays, and from when, is set by you and your buyer in your deal, for example in the purchase agreement or transition plan. The procedures your assistant documents belong to the business, so they transfer with it either way.

Who pays for the assistant, me or the buyer?

That’s for you and your buyer to decide as part of the deal. For example, the seller might cover the months before closing and the buyer the months after, or the two of you might agree something different in the purchase agreement or transition plan. VRA doesn’t advise on deal terms; your broker and attorney handle those.

I have run this for twenty years. Can someone really learn it?

Not all of it, and not the judgment. What can be captured is the repeatable part — the intake, the scheduling, the billing, the chasing, the exceptions you have explained to someone a hundred times. That is most of the hours and almost none of the irreplaceable bit.
