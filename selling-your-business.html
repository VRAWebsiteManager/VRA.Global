What You Are Actually Selling

A Buyer Is Not Paying for Last Year’s Revenue

They are paying for next year’s, and the only question that matters is whether it arrives without you in the building. Everything a buyer cannot verify, they assume the worst about and price accordingly.

What lifts the number

  • Procedures written down, in a form someone else can follow
  • Customer and supplier relationships that sit with the company
  • Administration that already happens without the owner touching it
  • A demonstrated stretch where you were not involved day to day
  • Questions in diligence that get answered with a document

What pulls it down

  • Pricing and exceptions that exist only in your memory
  • Accounts that stay because of you personally
  • An owner who has not taken a clear fortnight off in years
  • A handover that depends on how much you can remember out loud
  • A buyer doing sums on what it costs to replace you
The Part Nobody Warns You About

Four Ways Depending On You Follows You Past Closing Day

The discount is the visible cost. These are the ones owners discover afterwards.

1

A lower number

The most straightforward version. The buyer prices the risk that the business is really a job, and the offer reflects it.

2

Money held back

Part of the price gets tied to how the business performs after you leave — which is a strange bet to accept when the business has always run on you.

3

A longer stay

You agree to a few months of transition. It becomes a year, because nobody can answer the questions except you.

4

Carrying part of it yourself

If you are asked to finance a slice of the sale, you keep the exposure — and the new owner struggling is suddenly your problem again.

Before You Go to Market

Getting It Out of Your Head and Onto Paper

VRA trains assistants to work out how a business really operates and write it down. That training was built for exactly this moment.

What the assistant does

  • Sits alongside you and records how the work is actually done, exceptions included
  • Takes over the daily administration — telephones, diary, invoicing, follow-up
  • Keeps it running while you step back, which is the only real test of whether it was captured properly
  • Leaves behind written procedures that belong to the business, not to anyone’s memory
  • Carries on with the new owner afterwards, so the handover is a person as well as a folder

None of this requires you to decide today that you are definitely selling. It is useful the moment you start thinking about it, and it is useful if you change your mind.

Questions Owners Ask

Before You Start

How far ahead of a sale should this start?

Sooner is better, because the value is in having actually operated with you one step back rather than in the documents alone. Assistants run on a three-month commitment, so that is the practical floor — but owners who start a year out get something closer to a proven result than a recent change.

Is this confidential? We are only exploring the idea.

Yes. Every VRA assistant signs a confidentiality agreement and VRA will sign yours on request. Nothing about the engagement requires anyone to know you are considering a sale — from the inside it looks like hiring administrative help, because that is what it is.

Will my staff think I am replacing them?

The assistant administers the back office; they do not supervise anybody, and they are not a manager. In practice they take work off the people who have been absorbing it, which is usually you and whoever has been covering for you.

What if I decide not to sell after all?

Then you own a business that runs without you, which is the thing most owners wanted in the first place. Nothing about this only pays off at a sale.

Does the assistant go with the business?

That is the intention. The placement is designed to carry on with the new owner, so the person who learned how everything works stays with the company rather than leaving on the same day you do.

I have run this for twenty years. Can someone really learn it?

Not all of it, and not the judgement. What can be captured is the repeatable part — the intake, the scheduling, the billing, the chasing, the exceptions you have explained to someone a hundred times. That is most of the hours and almost none of the irreplaceable bit.
