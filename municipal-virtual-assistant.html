The Work Grows Faster Than the Headcount

The grant closed on a Friday. The city qualified for it. Nobody had the hours to notice it was open.

That is the shape of the problem in a small city. Work arrives continuously; capacity arrives on a budget cycle. Adding a staff member means a funding line, a posted role and often a council vote, so the gap gets carried by whoever has the least full week — until deadlines pass unclaimed, business visits go unlogged, and the monthly report gets written the night before. None of that is a staffing failure. It is a capacity problem with a procurement answer.

A municipal virtual assistant is a trained, contracted assistant who carries a city’s economic development, grant and administrative workload without the city creating a position.

FAQ

Questions City Staff Ask

What a VRA assistant covers across economic development, grants, communications and records.

What does a municipal virtual assistant do?

The work a small city never has enough hours for. On economic development: maintaining business databases, prospect lists and inquiry records, tracking development opportunities and incentives, researching local businesses, logging outreach visits and the concerns that come out of them, and preparing activity reports. On grants: researching federal, state and local funding opportunities, checking eligibility and deadlines, maintaining grant calendars, organizing supporting documents and monitoring reporting requirements. Across both: communications and newsletters, records and correspondence, accounts payable and reimbursement coordination, meeting scheduling and documentation, and monthly reporting.

Can an assistant help us find and apply for grants?

Yes, and for most small cities this is where the hours pay for themselves. Your assistant researches federal, state and local opportunities, checks eligibility and requirements, keeps a grant calendar so deadlines stop arriving unannounced, and organizes the supporting documents before they are needed. On the applications themselves they assist with drafting narratives, preparing budget information and coordinating submissions as assigned, then track the reporting requirements that follow an award. The money a small city misses is usually missed for lack of someone tracking the calendar, not for lack of eligibility.

How does this work with our procurement process?

It is a contracted service at a published flat rate, not a position to create, so it is procured the way a city procures any other vendor service. We have been through a municipal approval process already and can provide what your purchasing or legal review asks for: a signed confidentiality agreement, your own form of agreement where you require one, confirmation of our insurance coverage, and the name of the specific individual assigned to your office. Tell us what your process needs at the start and we will work to it.

Who approves anything the assistant publishes?

You do. Assistants draft, proofread and schedule, then follow the city’s own approval process before anything is published, exactly as your staff do. The assistant is there to get the newsletter, the website update or the outreach report ready and on time, not to decide what the city says.

Can they support economic development outreach?

Yes. Research on local businesses and maintaining outreach lists, tracking visits, communications, concerns and opportunities, coordinating meetings and follow-ups so inquiries do not sit, and preparing the outreach and activity reports your council or board expects. Business retention is largely a record-keeping and follow-up problem: knowing who you spoke to, what they raised and whether anybody went back. That is work an assistant is well suited to own.

Will they work in the systems we already use?

We train assistants on transferable fundamentals rather than one vendor’s product, and we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you. Tell us what your office runs during the Needs Assessment — the agenda or records platform, the accounting system, the website and newsletter tools — and we will match accordingly.

How quickly can an assistant start, and what do they cost?

Once you select your assistant, they can start within 24 hours of selection. Pricing is flat: $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week) or $800 a month for part-time (20 hours a week), with no setup or onboarding fees and no upcharge for specialization, subject to applicable agreement terms.
