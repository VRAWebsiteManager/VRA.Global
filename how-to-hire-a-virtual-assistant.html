A Practical Guide

How to Hire a Virtual Assistant

It is easy to stall before you start, because you are trying to decide who to hire before they have decided what to hand over. Do it the other way around and the rest is straightforward.

This is the order that works, what to ask any provider before you sign, and roughly how long it takes.

Write Down the Work, Not the Role

“I need an assistant” is not a brief. “Nobody confirms tomorrow’s appointments and nobody chases an invoice after day thirty” is.

What to list

  • Tasks that repeat weekly and do not need your judgment
  • The thing you do at 9pm that somebody else could do at 9am
  • Work that is currently late because it is nobody’s actual job
  • The question customers ask that you answer the same way every time

Where owners usually start

  • Inbox and calendar management
  • Appointment confirmations, reminders and reschedules
  • Invoicing, and chasing the receivables that have gone past due
  • CRM and database upkeep
  • Document collection and following up on missing items

Then Decide

Hours, and How You Want to Pay for Them

Part-time, around 20 hours

Enough for an inbox, a calendar and invoicing in most small businesses. The usual starting point when you are handing over your first process.

Full-time, around 40 hours

Right when there is phone coverage to hold during your business hours, or a second function to absorb — marketing, client service, documentation.

Hourly or per task

Looks cheaper and often is not. You end up rationing the hours, which means the work you most wanted off your desk is the work you keep doing yourself.

Published virtual assistant rates vary widely, and the way you buy — freelance hourly, agency hour blocks, or a monthly placement — moves the number more than anything else. What a virtual assistant actually costs →

Before You Sign

Six Questions Worth Asking Any VA Company

The answers vary more than the marketing does.

  • What training happens before placement, and how long is it? There is a real difference between a screened freelancer and someone who completed a program.
  • How many applicants actually make it through? A provider who knows the number will tell you it.
  • Who supervises the assistant day to day? It should be you. Be careful of anyone selling you a manager you did not ask for.
  • What happens if the fit is wrong? Ask whether there is a trial window, how long it runs, and what it costs to swap after it closes.
  • What is the commitment, and what changes at the end of it? Get the term and the exit in writing before the first invoice.
  • Is the price the price? Ask specifically about setup fees, overage, after-hours rates and bilingual upcharges.

Questions Owners Ask

Hiring a Virtual Assistant: Common Questions

How long does it take to hire a virtual assistant?

At VRA, you take the Needs Assessment, meet matched candidates on a Zoom and pick the one you want. Once you select your assistant, they can start within 24 hours of selection. The slowest part is deciding what to hand over, not the hiring.

What should I hand over first?

The work that repeats and does not need your judgment: inbox and calendar, appointment confirmations, invoicing and chasing the receivables that have gone past due, CRM and database upkeep, document collection. Owners who start with something vague like “help me with marketing” tend to stall. Owners who start with a repeating task get a result in the first two weeks and expand from there.

Do I have to train them myself?

You teach them your business; you should not have to teach them the job. Every VRA assistant completes a mandatory four-week, instructor-led program before any client work, covering CRM and database work, contract and e-signature workflows, and documenting procedures. On top of that we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you. The longer version: who pays to train your virtual assistant.

Full-time or part-time — which should I start with?

Part-time at 20 hours a week is enough for an inbox, a calendar and invoicing in most small businesses. Go full-time at 40 hours when there is phone coverage to hold or a second function to absorb. Increased coverage is available at any time.

What happens if it does not work out?

Ask any provider this before you sign, because the answers vary a lot. At VRA there are structured check-ins at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, and if the fit is wrong we swap in a different assistant at no extra cost. There is no trial window to miss and no fine print on the swap.

Who supervises the assistant day to day?

You do. A virtual assistant administers the back office under your direction — they carry out and take ownership of processes, but you still run the business and supervise any people. Access is provisioned by you, scoped to the work, and revocable at any time, and anything requiring a professional license stays with your licensed staff.
