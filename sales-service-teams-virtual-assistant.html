FAQ

Questions Sales and Service Teams Ask

What a VRA assistant covers on the phones, in the CRM, and through client onboarding — and where the line sits.

What does a sales and client service virtual assistant do?

On the sales side: outbound calling and appointment setting from your lead lists, speed-to-lead follow-up on inbound inquiries and web forms, CRM pipeline hygiene, nurture and no-show follow-up sequences, consultation scheduling, preparing proposals and quotes from your templates, and daily or weekly pipeline reporting. On the client service side: onboarding and welcome calls, intake paperwork, document collection and chasing missing items, case and file status tracking with proactive client updates, inbox and phone coverage during your business hours, invoicing follow-up and calls on past-due accounts, documenting how the desk runs as SOPs the team can follow, and post-service follow-up, review and referral requests.

Will an assistant actually call our leads, or just handle admin?

They call. Outbound calling, appointment setting, and speed-to-lead follow-up on inbound inquiries are core to the sales bench, alongside the CRM work that keeps the pipeline honest. Assistants work in your phone system and your CRM, with access you provision.

Can one assistant cover both sales and client service?

For a smaller team, often yes — and as volume grows most clients split the roles. One nationwide services firm runs a 15-person VRA bench across sales, client service, marketing and HR.

What stays with our licensed staff?

VRA virtual assistants provide administrative, marketing, and customer support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal, financial, or other advice requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by you and are subject to your own security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.
