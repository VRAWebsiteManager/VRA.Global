FAQ

Questions Restaurant Owners and Chefs Ask

What a VRA assistant covers across reservations, vendors, menus and reviews.

What does a restaurant virtual assistant do?

Reservations and confirmations in OpenTable, Resy or Tock; vendor coordination and purchase orders; menu and price updates in your point of sale and on the delivery apps; review replies across Google, Yelp and the delivery platforms; invoice entry and vendor list upkeep in QuickBooks; and draft staff schedules in 7shifts for your approval. Your assistant works inside the accounts you already have.

Can an assistant keep our menus and delivery apps in sync?

Yes. Menu, price and availability updates across delivery apps, your online ordering platform and your website are standard work, and they are the kind of task that slides to midnight.

Our busiest hours are nights and weekends. Does that work?

Your assistant’s hours are set by you, evenings or overnight if you need them, at the same flat rate with no after-hours upcharge. Weekend coverage can be requested; tell us the days you need and we will tell you what we can fill. What an assistant covers is everything around service — reservations and confirmations, phone and message coverage, ordering, menu updates, invoices and review responses.

Do you work with chefs and kitchen managers, or only owners?

Whoever carries the admin. In a small restaurant that may be the owner; in a larger kitchen it may be the chef or a kitchen manager fielding vendor calls, chasing invoices and pushing menu changes out. The work an assistant takes on is the same either way — ordering and vendor follow-up, reservations and confirmations, menu and price updates across delivery apps and your own site, invoicing, and review responses. Recipe development, costing calls and anything on the line stay with you.

Can a single-location restaurant afford this?

Yes. Part-time is $800 a month for 20 hours a week, full-time $1,500 for 40, flat either way with no after-hours upcharge. Reservations, vendor follow-up, menu and delivery-app updates and review responses do not need forty hours at a single site, so you are not buying a full-time salary to get the phone covered. If you are weighing it against a payroll hire, we set the two side by side.

Can an assistant answer the phone and take reservations for us?

Yes, and it is one of the most common first hand-offs. Your assistant takes reservation calls and booking requests, confirms the next day’s covers, works the waitlist and follows up on no-shows, and fields large-party and private-event inquiries, bringing you the details so you set the pricing. Calls forward to your assistant on the hours you choose, so the line stops pulling someone off the floor mid-service.

Will an assistant work in our POS, or do we have to change systems?

You keep what you run. Your assistant arrives able to work across the systems a restaurant runs on — point of sale, reservations, staff scheduling, bookkeeping and the delivery platforms — so the one you already use is the one they work in. Toast, Square or Clover at the counter; OpenTable, Resy or Tock on the book; 7shifts for schedules; QuickBooks for invoices. We commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

What access does an assistant need to our systems?

Whatever you decide to grant, and no more. You set the permissions in each account — a reservation platform login, a delivery-app dashboard, a shared inbox — at the level the work needs. VRA assistants sign a confidentiality agreement, and VRA will sign your own NDA on request. Placements are backed by a $1 million Errors & Omissions policy. If you would rather start narrow, give your assistant one system and widen it once you have seen the work.

We already use an answering service. How is an assistant different?

An answering service picks up the phone. An assistant carries the task to the end — books the table, updates the menu that caused the question, chases the vendor who missed the delivery and replies to the review that came out of it. The same named person does it each day, so they learn your room, your regulars and your standards. And the hours are yours to set, evenings included, so the coverage sits where your service actually is. We walk through where a person beats a phone tool, and where it does not.

Can an assistant handle delivery-app disputes and missing-item claims?

Yes, as administration. Your assistant pulls the order record, gathers the ticket and the timestamps, files the dispute in the DoorDash, Uber Eats or Grubhub dashboard and tracks it until it closes, then reports what came back. Decisions about comps, refunds and goodwill stay with you. It is steady, unglamorous work that few owners get to before the end of the month, which is usually when the window to claim has closed.
