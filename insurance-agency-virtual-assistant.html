What Your VA Can Do

Virtual Assistant Services for Insurance Agencies

A VRA insurance agency assistant handles the administrative workload behind your book, under the direction of your licensed staff. For the service-account side in detail, here is how policy checking, endorsements and renewal prep actually get worked.

Certificates, Renewals & Policy Admin

  • Certificates of insurance (COIs) — preparing and issuing standard certificates from existing policy data, per your agency’s procedures, with additional-insured or non-standard wording routed to licensed staff
  • Renewal prep and reminders — pulling upcoming renewals from your AMS, sending client reminders, gathering updated exposure information and assembling the file for your account manager
  • Endorsement processing paperwork — preparing the paperwork for changes your licensed staff have approved, and tracking carrier confirmations through to the file
  • ACORD applications and forms — completed from the information already collected, ready for your producer to review and sign off
  • Loss runs — ordered from the carrier and chased until they arrive, so the file is complete before anyone needs it
  • Remarketing support at renewal — gathering updated exposure information, assembling the submission and preparing the comparison for your producer to present
  • Policy checking — comparing the issued policy against the quote, the application and the prior declarations page, and flagging anything that differs for your licensed staff to resolve

AMS, Follow-Up & Client Service

  • Data entry into your agency management system — Applied Epic, AMS360, HawkSoft, EZLynx, QQ Catalyst or Better Agency, whichever you run — covering new clients, policy details, documents, activities and notes, kept clean and current
  • Gathering quote information and applications — sending intake forms, collecting client details and documents, chasing what’s missing and entering it so your producer can quote
  • Follow-ups — outstanding documents, signatures, carrier requests and open items, tracked until they close
  • Phone coverage during your business hours — answering and routing service calls, taking messages and logging the request in your AMS, with anything about coverage routed to licensed staff
  • Client service email — routine requests such as ID cards, document copies and status updates, with anything about coverage routed to licensed staff
  • Carrier downloads — processing the daily feed, matching documents to the right client record, attaching them, and flagging anything that doesn’t match what the AMS says
  • Direct bill reconciliation — matching commission statements, supporting invoicing and following up on payments that haven’t landed
  • Scheduling — calendar management, client reviews and renewal meetings for your producers

Marketing & Client Communication

  • Social media management and content scheduling
  • Renewal and cross-sell email campaigns, prepared for your approval
  • Review requests and referral follow-up
  • Client newsletters and mailings
  • Flyers, social graphics and one-pagers in Canva

Marketing material is prepared for your approval and makes no statements about coverage, rates or carrier terms.

Spanish-speaking clients? A bilingual assistant can handle client email, document collection and follow-up in Spanish or English, at the same rate.

Licensed Work Stays Licensed

What Your VA Handles, and What Stays With Your Licensed Staff

Insurance is a licensed business, and agencies are right to be careful about who talks to clients about coverage. Here is exactly where the line is drawn. The same question comes up with software, and we wrote it up separately: which AI tools are worth running in an agency, and which work is better delegated to a person.

What Your VA Handles

  • COIs, renewal prep and reminders, and follow-ups
  • AMS data entry and endorsement paperwork
  • Collecting quote information and applications from clients
  • Scheduling, routine client service email and marketing support

What Stays With Your Licensed Staff

  • No quoting — the assistant gathers the information; your producer prepares and presents the quote
  • No selling — no soliciting, negotiating or recommending a policy or carrier
  • No binding — no binding, issuing or changing coverage on the assistant’s own authority
  • No advising on coverage — no explaining, interpreting or recommending coverage, limits or deductibles to a client

VRA virtual assistants provide administrative and operational support under the direction of your agency. They do no licensed work: no quoting, selling, binding, or advising on coverage. When a client asks a coverage question, the assistant routes it to your licensed staff and keeps the file moving on the admin side. Access and permissions are determined by your agency and are subject to your carrier agreements, E&O requirements, and your security, confidentiality and compliance procedures.

FAQ

Questions Agency Owners Ask

What a VRA assistant does in an independent agency, where the licensed-work line sits, and what it costs.

What does an insurance agency virtual assistant do?

A VRA insurance agency virtual assistant handles the administrative side of your agency: certificates of insurance (COIs), renewal prep and reminders, follow-ups with clients and carriers, data entry into your agency management system (AMS), endorsement processing paperwork, scheduling, client service email, and gathering quote information and applications from clients. Some agencies also use their assistant for marketing and social media. The assistant works under the direction of your licensed staff, who keep all licensed work.

Can a VRA assistant quote, sell, bind or advise our clients on coverage?

No. VRA assistants do no licensed work. They do not quote, sell, bind, or advise on coverage, and they do not interpret a policy for a client. Those stay with your licensed producers and account managers. When a client asks a coverage question, the assistant routes it to your licensed staff and keeps the file moving on the admin side.

Will the assistant work inside our agency management system?

Yes. Your assistant works inside the AMS and carrier portals your agency already uses, such as Applied Epic, AMS360, HawkSoft or EZLynx, on access you provision, scope and can revoke. What access is appropriate is set by your agency, subject to your carrier agreements and E&O requirements. Your assistant arrives knowing how to work client records, notes and follow-up tasks, learns your agency's insurance-specific workflows with you, and we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

Can the assistant help with our marketing?

Yes. Agencies use their assistant for social media management, renewal and cross-sell email campaigns, review requests, referral follow-up and client newsletters. Marketing material is prepared for your approval and makes no statements about coverage, rates or carrier terms, so the work stays on the administrative side of the line.

Do you offer bilingual assistants for Texas and Florida agencies?

Yes. If your clients speak Spanish, we can match you with a Spanish-English bilingual assistant at the same flat rate, with no bilingual upcharge. The assistant can handle client service email, document collection, follow-ups and scheduling in whichever language your client prefers.

How are VRA assistants screened and trained?

Assistants come through screening, a mandatory 4-week, instructor-led training program and a final evaluation before they start client work. In our latest intake, 1,800 applications produced 16 graduating assistants, or 0.89% of our latest applicant cohort. Matching is based on your Needs Assessment, and candidates are screened with a DISC Assessment and a Keller Personality Assessment for workplace culture and fast-learning ability. Identity is verified against a government-issued photo ID on a live video call during onboarding.

What does an insurance agency virtual assistant cost?

$1,500 a month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week) or $800 a month for a part-time assistant (20 hours a week). Flat rate, invoiced on the 20th of each month, with no setup fee, no overage charges and no upcharge for a bilingual assistant, subject to applicable agreement terms.

We are a small agency. Does part-time make sense for us?

Part-time exists for exactly this. Agencies with a handful of staff often have enough COIs, renewals and AMS cleanup to fill 20 hours a week well before they can justify another salary. $800 a month covers 20 hours a week with your own named assistant, and moving up to full-time later books your assistant's whole week to you.
