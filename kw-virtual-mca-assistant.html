What a Virtual MCA Assistant Actually Is

Market center leaders search for a “virtual MCA.” The Market Center Administrator is an in-office staff role and not one that can be filled virtually, so that is not what this is. A Virtual MCA Assistant works for the MCA you already have: the CDAs, the receivables, the agent billing and the Command upkeep, entered and followed up on by someone whose whole day is that work.

FAQ

Questions Market Center Leaders Ask About the Seat

What the role covers, where its limits are, and how fast it starts working.

What does a Virtual MCA Assistant actually do all day?

A Virtual MCA Assistant spends most of the day on the commission pipeline and the receivables. CDAs get created and uploaded in KW Command, with commission splits, deductions and mentor fees entered for the MCA to review, and revised when a deal changes. Receivables get worked as a list rather than as whoever comes to mind — at Keller Williams Memorial that is 60 to 75-plus agents a month. Agent billing and office bill invoicing run monthly in AccountEdge, and agent onboarding and offboarding get entered in the books and across the other systems the office runs. It is high-volume procedural work, which is why it goes badly when it is squeezed between interruptions.

What can a market center safely outsource, and what stays in the office?

The procedural half of a market center back office travels well: entry, uploads, revisions, receivables outreach, invoicing, record upkeep and reporting. The half that stays is the half that needs authority or judgment — review and approval, exceptions and write-offs, personnel matters, and the harder conversation when a receivable does not come in. The split is already built into how the work runs: the assistant enters the CDA, the MCA reviews it. Your MCA and leadership team still run the market center.

Does the assistant need access to our accounting system and KW Command?

A Virtual MCA Assistant needs access to KW Command and your accounting system, at the permission level you decide. VRA assistants already work in KW Command and AccountEdge at market centers, alongside Courted, Zoho and the agent in-process and out-process systems. Access is granted by your office, scoped by your office and removed by your office. Every VRA assistant is backed by a $1 million Errors & Omissions insurance policy, and we will have an assistant sign an NDA whenever one is requested.

How long before a new assistant is useful on CDAs?

We commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you. Mastering one market center’s workflow means picking up another quickly, which is why that number is days rather than months. Check-ins follow at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, and if the fit is not right you can swap in a different assistant at any point at no extra cost.

Can one assistant cover CDAs and accounts receivable, or does that take two?

One assistant can carry both CDAs and accounts receivable at the volumes we have seen. At Keller Williams Memorial a single assistant handles over 400 CDAs in a month and works a receivables list of 60 to 75-plus agents. Where a market center adds a second assistant, it is usually for a different seat — agent services, marketing, recruiting, education and events — rather than to split the accounting.

Do we have to be a Keller Williams market center?

A market center does not have to be Keller Williams. The vocabulary on this page is Keller Williams because that is where the work started — VRA was founded by a licensed Texas broker who leads the Keller Williams Memorial market center. Independent brokerages and other franchises run the same back office under different names: commissions get disbursed, agents get billed, receivables get chased, and new agents get set up across a dozen systems. The systems differ and the work does not.
