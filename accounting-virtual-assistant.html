FAQ

Questions Accountants Ask

What an assistant does, where the licensed work stops, how access is handled, and what it costs.

What does an accounting virtual assistant do?

The administrative work around the engagement rather than the engagement itself: collecting and chasing client documents, scheduling appointments and managing calendars, client intake and onboarding coordination, keeping your CRM and client database current, tracking deadlines and task lists, invoicing and calling on past-due client invoices, writing standard operating procedures (SOPs) for the practice, inbox management, review and referral requests, and marketing support. The work that eats a partner’s day without requiring a license.

Do they prepare or review tax returns?

No. VRA virtual assistants provide administrative support under the direction of the client. They do not prepare, review or sign returns, give tax, accounting or financial advice, or perform work requiring a CPA license or a PTIN. The professional work stays with your licensed staff; the administrative load around it is what an assistant takes on.

Can an assistant work in our tax software and client portal?

They work in the systems you already use, with access you provision and can revoke at any time. VRA assistants are trained on transferable fundamentals rather than one vendor’s product, and we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

Our client documents contain sensitive information. How is access handled?

Access and permissions are determined by you, scoped to what the work requires, and revocable at any time. Assistants work inside your systems rather than exporting anything to a separate platform, and the arrangement is subject to your own security, confidentiality and compliance procedures.

What happens during filing season?

Your assistant works the schedule you set — 40 hours a week full-time, 20 part-time, evenings and overnight included if filing season needs it. Most practices use the quieter months to get their assistant fluent in the workflow, so that when deadlines stack up the document chasing, scheduling and client follow-up is already off the partners’ desks. Increased coverage is available at any time.

Is the assistant assigned only to us?

You are matched with one named assistant who learns your process, rather than a rotating pool. A full-time assistant works for your business and no one else; a part-time assistant is your own named assistant with reserved hours. Matching starts with a two-minute Needs Assessment, and every assistant is screened with a DISC Assessment and a Keller Personality Assessment for fit and fast-learning ability.

Do you work with solo accountants, or only larger firms?

Both. A sole practitioner and a multi-partner firm need different amounts of the same work, which is what the two plans are for — full-time at 40 hours a week for a practice with enough volume to keep an assistant busy through the year, part-time at 20 hours for a smaller book or a first attempt at delegating. The rate is flat either way, $1,500 a month full-time and $800 part-time.

What should a practice delegate first?

Document chasing. It is the task that most reliably delays a return, it needs no license, and nobody in the practice enjoys it. After that, scheduling and the past-due invoice follow-up that partners keep meaning to get to. Hand over one process, watch it get documented, then hand over the next.
