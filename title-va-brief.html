VIRTUAL REALTY ASSISTANTS
For Title Company Managers

A Trained Title VA, Without Adding Headcount

Title is consolidating through one of the hardest real estate cycles the industry has seen, and offices are being asked to close the same files with fewer people. A VRA virtual assistant is trained in Qualia before day one and works under your direction — more capacity at a lower cost than a hire.

What Your VA Does, Day to Day

  • Imports title commitments from Marketplace into the file, and hyperlinks them for easy reference.
  • Pre-closing file review and checklist support — commitments, buyer, seller and lender information, tax certificates, tax statements and payoff statements.
  • Administrative updates to Schedule C after entity documents have been reviewed and approved by the appropriate title professional or underwriter.
  • Underwriter coordination — preparing and sending buyer and seller documents for review and approval.
  • Lender coordination — gathering and delivering the documents lenders require.
  • Marketing support, where a county office needs it — social media management that keeps you visible to agents and lenders.

Who’s Already Doing It

An escrow operations president who advises Texas title agencies on compliance started with one VRA virtual assistant. She now has three, and has referred clients of her own to VRA.
A Texas title company president had questions at the start. At her 60-day check-in: “She’s a self-starter and she’s finding things that we didn’t expect her really to find.”
At another title company, a VRA virtual assistant runs social media management — without pulling closers and processors off their files.

Why the Work Holds Up

  • A mandatory 4-week, instructor-led training program before any client work — including Qualia for VAs headed to title teams.
  • The closing workflow is the same in any title production system — if your office runs SoftPro or another platform, your VA works in yours.
  • VAs supporting Texas title clients are also trained on common Texas real estate contract documents, timelines and file-review procedures relevant to their administrative role.
  • Exceptional English communication skills are a requirement to be hired at VRA.
  • Only the top 1% of applicants make it through screening, hands-on training and a final evaluation.
  • Check-ins at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, with the option to swap in a different VA at any point, at no extra cost.

What It Costs

$1,500/month Full-time VA — 40 hours/week, 1-year agreement.
$800/month Part-time VA — 20 hours/week, 6-month agreement.

Flat rate, no hidden fees, subject to applicable agreement terms.

How this works: VRA virtual assistants provide administrative and operational support under the direction of the client. They do not provide legal advice, underwriting decisions, escrow authority, or services requiring professional licensure. Access and permissions are determined by the client and are subject to the client’s security, confidentiality, and compliance procedures.
