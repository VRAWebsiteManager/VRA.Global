Almost none of this work is anybody’s whole job. It sits on top of what the staff was already doing, which is why it is the first thing to go when the week fills up. Agent services is data entry done by someone sitting where agents walk up with questions — the interruptions are the job, and so is the entry, and asking one person to do both well is asking them to be in two places. Recruiting and recognition slip differently: no single day is the day they matter, and then a production award gets announced a week late, or three names get left off the list. Agents notice that faster than they notice almost anything else the office does. Event sign-ups are a third version of the same problem. A First Step session has to fill a room bigger than your training room in order to fill your training room for BOLD, and that takes weeks of steady promotion — steady being the one thing a stretched week cannot produce. It is also uncomfortable to ask an agent you see every morning to pay for a class, which is its own reason the asking gets put off.

None of it is a shortage of effort, and none of it needs another desk. What it needs is someone attached to the work whose day does not get interrupted by the front counter.

And the hours are not really the point. A market center’s culture gets built by the people in it — the conversation in the hallway, the agent who gets noticed during a hard month, the class that feels worth showing up to. That is work only the people in the building can do, and paperwork is the first thing that takes them away from it. Covering the back office is how your staff gets to spend their attention on the office instead of on the queue.

FAQ

Questions Market Center Leadership Asks

What a VRA assistant covers across MC accounting, agent services, recruiting support and marketing.

What does a VRA assistant do for a market center?

Commission Disbursement Authorization (CDA) processing, accounts receivable outreach and collections follow-up, agent billing and invoicing support, KW Command database management, Agent Services data entry and in-processing support, agent onboarding and offboarding across websites and systems, Preferred Partner Program coordination, calendar and inbox management for leadership, agent-facing communication on the market center’s behalf, MC marketing support, social scheduling, recruiting and agent-attraction support including pipeline tracking and production reporting, commercial and business brokerage transaction coordination, luxury division marketing, and class, event and training coordination.

Is this a “virtual MCA”?

A Virtual MCA Assistant and a Market Center Administrator are two different things. The Market Center Administrator is an in-office staff role and not one that can be filled virtually, so it is not a role we fill. A Virtual MCA Assistant is an assistant to the MCA you already have, handling CDA processing, accounts receivable follow-up, agent billing, agent onboarding paperwork and KW Command upkeep. At Keller Williams Memorial, our assistant handles the CDAs — over 400 in a single month — so the MCA’s hours go to agents and staff instead of paperwork.

Do you already work with market centers, or just individual agents?

VRA works with market centers and with individual agents. Our assistants support Keller Williams Memorial and Keller Williams Heritage in San Antonio across accounting, marketing and special projects. One market center runs ten VRA assistants across its back office — that story is written up in our Insights.

Can an assistant support recruiting and agent attraction?

An assistant can own recruiting and agent-attraction support: pipeline tracking, appointment coordination and production reporting. The judgment calls and the conversations with agents stay with your leadership team.

Can we cover the back office without adding a staff position?

CDA processing, accounts receivable follow-up, agent billing, Command and database upkeep, and the in-processing and out-processing that runs across a dozen systems. All of it is high-volume, procedural and detail-heavy, and none of it needs a seat in the office. An assistant administers that work under your direction; your MCA and leadership team still run the market center.

What changes for the market center once the back office is covered?

The measurable part is throughput: CDAs go out without waiting on someone’s free hour, receivables get worked as a list, new agents get set up the week they join, and the award announcements and recognition posts go out on time with nobody left off. The part leaders tend to care about more is what the staff does with the attention that comes back. Culture gets built by the people in the building — the hallway conversation, the agent who gets noticed during a hard month, the class worth showing up to — and that is the first thing a full queue crowds out.

Can an assistant handle our market center bookkeeping?

Market center bookkeeping is work an assistant can take, and it is why we built a separate certification for it. Assistants who add our Business, Finance & Bookkeeping certification train on bookkeeping fundamentals, QuickBooks ProAdvisor coursework and payroll on top of the standard four weeks — the financial side of running an office, not just the commission pipeline. Agent billing and office bill invoicing run monthly in AccountEdge. The review and the approvals stay with your MCA.

What reporting can an assistant own, and on what cadence?

The reporting an assistant owns at a market center runs weekly and monthly. Weekly is agent-in and agent-out. Monthly is the recruiting report, agent billing and office bill invoicing, and the production numbers behind recognition. Your office sets the cadence and the format — an assistant builds the report on the schedule you already keep rather than inventing one — and the read on what the numbers mean stays with leadership.
