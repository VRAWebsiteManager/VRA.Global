Why Does Coordination Eat the Week It Eats?

Because almost none of it is hard, and all of it is time-sensitive. A lender needs a document the seller has. An inspector can come Thursday but the listing agent has not confirmed. The option period ends Friday and nobody has said so out loud. Each item takes four minutes and arrives at a moment you were doing something else.

Which is the real cost. Not the hours, but what the hours interrupt. An agent who spends the afternoon chasing an HOA resale certificate is an agent who did not follow up on the listing appointment.

A transaction coordinator virtual assistant is a trained, contracted assistant who takes the signed contract and carries the file to close — tracking the dates, collecting the documents and keeping every party informed — without the agent creating a position or paying per file.

What Your Coordinator Does

What a Transaction Coordinator Handles, Stage by Stage

Split by where a file actually is. Going deeper: what a transaction coordinator does from contract to close · the same work on a commercial deal

Opening the File

  • Executed contract out to every party the same day
  • Title or escrow opened and the file number circulated
  • Earnest money and option fee delivery confirmed and receipted
  • Every date calendared, with internal reminders ahead of each
  • Introduction email so each party knows who to ask for what
  • File built to your broker’s compliance checklist from day one

Through the Contingencies

  • Inspection, appraisal access and repair negotiation scheduling
  • HOA documents, survey and resale certificate ordered and chased
  • Lender document requests routed and followed to delivery
  • Amendments and addenda out for signature and filed on return
  • Deadline status to the agent before a date lapses, not after
  • Buyer, seller, co-operating agent, lender and title kept current

Closing and Afterwards

  • Closing disclosure and settlement statement circulated
  • Final walkthrough and utility transfer coordination
  • Commission disbursement paperwork prepared to your office’s process
  • Compliance file completed and submitted for broker review
  • Closed file archived where your office keeps it
  • Post-close follow-up and review request on your behalf

Reserved Hours, Not a Price Per File

Transaction coordination is often bought by the file, which means the cost of a good month and the cost of a bad month are the same problem in different directions. Twelve closings produce twelve charges. Two closings leave you paying for nothing in between.

A VRA assistant is reserved hours at a flat rate: $1,500 a month for 40 hours a week, or $800 a month for 20. Volume does not change the invoice. And in the hours between closings the same assistant takes listing document prep, MLS and CRM upkeep, buyer and seller communication, open house scheduling and marketing support — so a slow month converts into pipeline work rather than idle capacity.

FAQ

Questions Agents and Team Leaders Ask

What a VRA coordinator covers from contract to close, and how it is priced.

What does a transaction coordinator virtual assistant do?

A transaction coordinator virtual assistant takes a signed contract and carries the file to close so the agent can stay in front of clients. The work is opening the file and getting the contract to every party, tracking the dates that carry consequences, collecting documents and chasing the ones that do not arrive, scheduling inspections, appraisal access and the final walkthrough, keeping buyer, seller, co-operating agent, lender and title updated, assembling the broker compliance file, and closing the file out afterwards. It is coordination and records work rather than advice: the agent still advises the client and negotiates the deal.

How many files can one coordinator carry?

That depends on your contract, your market and how much of the file the assistant owns, so any number quoted without seeing your process is a guess. What matters more than the count is that the hours are reserved rather than sold per file. A full-time assistant is 40 hours a week for $1,500 a month whether you close two deals that month or twelve, so a heavy month does not produce a bigger invoice.

Is this priced per transaction?

No. Pricing is flat: $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant at 40 hours a week, or $800 a month part-time at 20 hours, with no setup or onboarding fees and no per-file charge. If you are comparing against per-file coordination, the difference is that a flat rate stops scaling with your volume, and the same assistant can take listing prep, CRM upkeep and client communication in the hours between closings instead of sitting idle.

Do they know my state's contracts?

Assistants supporting transaction coordination are trained on the documents, timelines and file-review steps a coordinator works through from contract to close, with state-specific contract training for Texas and Florida. For other states, we train to your forms and your broker’s file requirements during onboarding, and we commit to 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

Which systems will they work in?

Whatever you already run. Assistants complete instructor-led training in e-signature workflows in dotloop and DocuSign, scheduling and database management and document preparation before they are placed, and we train on transferable fundamentals rather than one vendor’s product. Tell us your transaction platform, your CRM and your broker’s compliance system during the Needs Assessment and we will match accordingly.

Can the same assistant do more than coordination?

Yes, and most do. Once the file work is running, the same assistant typically picks up listing document prep, MLS and CRM upkeep, buyer and seller communication, open house scheduling and marketing support. You are buying reserved hours rather than a single function, so the role grows with what you hand over.

How quickly can a coordinator start, and what does it cost?

Once you select your assistant, they can start within 24 hours of selection. Pricing is flat: $1,500 a month for a full-time assistant (40 hours a week) or $800 a month for part-time (20 hours a week), with no hidden fees and no upcharge for specialization, subject to applicable agreement terms.
