FAQ

Questions Executives Ask

What the role covers, how it differs from general admin, and who we place it with.

What does an executive virtual assistant do?

An executive assistant works close to one person rather than across a whole office. Inbox ownership and triage, calendar and scheduling, travel coordination, meeting prep and notes, drafting correspondence, chasing the follow-ups you do not have time to chase, keeping contacts and the CRM current, and screening what reaches you. Behind that sits the same admin bench the rest of VRA runs on: vendor and invoice tracking, bookkeeping support in QuickBooks, reporting, document organization and project tracking.

How is this different from general admin or back office support?

Admin and back office support keep the operation moving. An executive assistant keeps one person moving. The task list overlaps, but the orientation is different: an executive assistant learns how you decide, what you want to see and what you never want to see again, then works ahead of you rather than behind you. The rate is the same either way, so the question is which shape you need, not which costs more. Tell us in the Needs Assessment and we match to it.

What is the difference between an executive assistant and a virtual assistant?

They are not opposites, and the comparison trips people up. "Virtual assistant" describes where someone works. "Executive assistant" describes who they work for and how closely. A VRA executive assistant is both: a trained assistant working remotely, assigned to one person, who learns how that person decides rather than waiting to be told. What people usually mean by the question is whether remote can really hold the role, and the answer turns on ownership. An assistant who merely watches your inbox is remote support. An assistant who answers what they can answer, routes what they cannot and brings you the rest with the context attached is an executive assistant, wherever they sit.

Do you place executive assistants outside real estate?

Yes. Real estate is where VRA started, and an executive real estate virtual assistant is still the most common placement, but the role itself is not industry-specific. We place executive assistants with title and escrow principals, mortgage and lending offices, professional services firms, and founders and operating executives outside real estate entirely. Inbox, calendar, travel, follow-up and reporting look the same whatever is written on the door.

Can an executive assistant own my inbox and calendar?

Yes, and it is usually the first thing handed over. Your assistant triages what arrives, answers what they can answer, routes what they cannot, and brings you the rest with the context attached. The same applies to the calendar: booking, rescheduling, protecting the blocks you need and preparing you for what is next. Access is provisioned by you and revocable at any time.

Is it safe to give an assistant access to my inbox and accounts?

Access is only ever what you grant, and it stays revocable. Every VRA assistant’s identity is verified against a government-issued photo ID on a live video call as part of onboarding, and the name on that ID is matched to their application and payment records. Every assistant signs a confidentiality agreement with VRA before they are placed, and VRA will sign your own non-disclosure agreement on request. VRA also carries a $1 million Errors & Omissions policy. For an executive handover, a good start is one mailbox or one calendar through a shared password manager or permission-based access, widened as the working relationship settles.

How much does a virtual executive assistant cost?

$1,500 a month for a full-time assistant at 40 hours a week, and $800 a month for part-time at 20 hours, invoiced on the 20th. It is a flat monthly rate rather than an hourly one, and it is the same rate as any other VRA assistant, with no upcharge for specialized talent, subject to applicable agreement terms. The rate covers reserved hours, matching and onboarding, and check-ins at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, and if the fit is wrong you can swap in a different assistant at any point at no extra cost. Compared against a local executive assistant hire, the monthly figure is the whole figure: no payroll taxes, benefits, equipment or recruiting fee sitting underneath it.

Will the same assistant stay with me, or do I get whoever is free?

You are matched with one assistant and that is who you work with. A full-time assistant works for you exclusively. A part-time assistant is reserved for your hours each week. Matching starts with the Needs Assessment and a DISC and Keller Personality Assessment on every candidate, because for this role fit with one person matters more than it does anywhere else on the bench.
