Data & Security Boundaries

What Your Mortgage VA Can (and Can’t) Access

Mortgage offices are right to be careful about who touches borrower data. Here’s exactly where the line is drawn — and why it’s there.

Marketing & Client Communication

  • Social media management and content scheduling
  • CRM-based borrower nurture campaigns
  • Referral marketing to your agent and partner referral sources
  • Review requests and follow-up
  • Flyers, social graphics and one-pagers in Canva

Marketing material is prepared for your approval and makes no statements about rates or loan terms.

What Your VA Handles

  • Manage your calendar, CRM, and loan pipeline database
  • Handle borrower follow-up calls, emails, and appointment scheduling
  • Build and run social media, email, and ad campaigns
  • Handle general admin — data entry, inbox management, document organization, invoicing and reporting

What Stays With Your Licensed Staff

  • No LOS access — the assistant does not log into your Loan Origination System
  • No borrower financial data — no collecting, storing or viewing Social Security numbers, credit reports or other sensitive borrower information
  • No rate or product advice — no quoting rates, discussing loan terms, or advising a borrower on which loan product to choose
  • No loan file work — no underwriting, loan file setup or condition-clearing

That boundary isn’t a limitation — it keeps your VA outside the systems that carry sensitive borrower data, so the work they do sits well away from your loan file.

FAQ

Questions Lending Offices Ask

What a VRA assistant does in a mortgage office, where the line sits around borrower data, and what it costs.

What does a mortgage virtual assistant do?

A VRA mortgage and lending VA handles borrower communication and follow-up, appointment scheduling and calendar management, CRM and pipeline database management, invoicing and reporting support, general admin support, and social media and marketing campaign support — the day-to-day workload of a busy lending office, without touching sensitive loan file work.

Does a mortgage virtual assistant have access to my borrower’s Social Security number or loan file?

No. VRA’s mortgage and lending virtual assistants provide administrative and marketing support only — scheduling, CRM management, borrower follow-up, and marketing campaigns. They do not log into a Loan Origination System (LOS), and do not collect, view or store sensitive borrower data such as Social Security numbers, credit reports, or loan file documents.

Does a mortgage virtual assistant need to be SOC 2 compliant?

VRA is not SOC 2 certified. SOC 2 is generally scoped to vendors who handle sensitive borrower data inside systems like a Loan Origination System, and VRA’s mortgage assistants are not given LOS access — they don’t collect, view or store Social Security numbers, credit reports or loan file documents. Access and permissions are set by you and can be revoked at any time, and every engagement is backed by VRA’s $1 million Errors & Omissions insurance policy. Whether that satisfies your vendor management policy is your call to make.

What if my VA needs a skill we didn’t anticipate?

That is what the check-ins are for. If a 14, 30, 60 or 90-day check-in surfaces a skill your office needs, VRA gives your assistant access to the matching course and trains them in it — so you keep the person who already knows your pipeline instead of starting over with someone new.

Do you work with independent mortgage brokers, or only larger lending offices?

Both. A VRA assistant works the same way for a solo mortgage broker as for a multi-person lending office — borrower follow-up, pipeline admin, scheduling and marketing, under your direction. Smaller brokerages often get more out of it, because there is nobody else absorbing the admin. Pricing does not change with the size of the office: $1,500 a month full-time or $800 part-time, flat.

Is this the same as a loan officer assistant?

It is the unlicensed half of that role. A VRA assistant does the administrative and marketing work a loan officer assistant does — loan pipeline tracking, borrower follow-up and appointment scheduling, referral partner communication, CRM hygiene, invoicing and reporting. What they do not do is the licensed half: no taking applications, no collecting income, asset or credit details, no LOS access, and no underwriting or condition-clearing work. Every VRA mortgage assistant completes a four-module program that includes compliance boundaries for unlicensed staff, so that line is trained rather than assumed.

How quickly can a mortgage virtual assistant start?

Once you select your assistant, they can start within 24 hours of selection. Before that, matching begins with the Needs Assessment and a DISC and Keller Personality Assessment on every candidate, and you interview the ones we put forward. An assistant reaches 90% proficiency in your systems within 5 business days of starting with you.

How much does a mortgage virtual assistant cost?

$1,500 a month for a full-time assistant at 40 hours a week, and $800 a month for part-time at 20 hours. It is a flat monthly rate rather than an hourly one, with no hidden fees and no upcharge for industry specialization. The rate covers reserved hours, matching and onboarding, and check-ins at 14, 30, 60 and 90 days, and if the fit is wrong you can swap in a different assistant at any point at no extra cost.

How do mortgage virtual assistant services differ in turnaround time?

Mostly by who else is in the queue. Where assistants are shared across several clients, your work waits behind theirs, and turnaround depends on how busy the pool is that week. VRA places one assistant with one office, and a full-time assistant works for you exclusively, so the limit is the hours you reserve rather than someone else’s backlog. Coverage runs standard US business hours on a schedule you set, with evenings and overnight available on request at the same rate and no after-hours upcharge.

We need help but cannot afford another full-time hire. What are the options?

Part-time exists for exactly this. $800 a month covers 20 hours a week and $1,500 covers 40, flat, with the same matching process and the same trained assistant either way. An office can start at twenty hours against one busy season, and increased coverage is available at any time.
